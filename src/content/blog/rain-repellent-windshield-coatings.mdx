Rain repellent produces unusually polarised opinions — people either swear by it or say it
does nothing. Both groups are describing accurate experiences of different driving.

## Speed is the whole variable

These products work by making the glass hydrophobic, so water beads instead of sheeting.

**Above roughly 40mph**, airflow across the screen carries those beads away. In steady
motorway rain you can often drive with the wipers off entirely, and the improvement in
night visibility — no smearing, no wiper streaks through oncoming headlights — is
genuinely significant.

**At town speeds** there isn't enough airflow to move the beads. They sit there, and you
wipe as normal.

That's the entire disagreement. It's a motorway product, and people who mostly drive in
town accurately report that it changed nothing.

## Clean glass first, or it causes problems

The most common complaint after applying rain repellent is juddering wipers. That's almost
always an application problem rather than the product.

Glass that looks clean usually carries road film, old repellent residue and bonded
contamination. Coating over that gives you a surface with **inconsistent friction**, which
is exactly what makes a wiper skip and chatter.

Decontaminate properly first — a clay pass on the glass, or a dedicated glass polish, then

## Remove the old coating before reapplying

This is the step almost nobody does, and it's why second applications disappoint more than
first ones.

A partially worn coating leaves patchy friction. Applying fresh product over it doesn't
reset that — you're layering onto an uneven base. The wiped arc degrades far faster than
the edges, so coverage goes blotchy rather than fading evenly.

Use a glass polish or fine abrasive cleaner to take the old one back off first.

## Sound blades or don't bother

Rain repellent doesn't damage wipers, but it exposes blades that are already marginal.
Juddering that appears with a coating often means the blades were on their way out
anyway.

The diagnostic for whether a blade is genuinely finished is in
[when to replace windshield wipers](/blog/when-to-replace-windshield-wipers/).

## Spray or ceramic glass coating?

A spray lasts weeks to a few months and costs very little. A ceramic glass coating lasts a
year or more with stronger water behaviour.

The trade-off is the same one that applies to paint: **prep is most of the job**, and a
durable coating bonded to contaminated glass locks in the problem. The same reasoning that
makes coating wheels a good return is in
[ceramic coating wheels](/blog/do-ceramic-coatings-work-on-wheels/).

## Where it earns its money

Night motorway driving in rain. That's the case where it's genuinely transformative rather
than marginal — the combination of beading, no wiper smear, and no oncoming-headlight
glare through a wiped arc.

If that's not your driving, it's a nice-to-have rather than an upgrade.
