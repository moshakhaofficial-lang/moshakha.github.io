We set out to write a buying guide for car air purifiers. What the research turned up was
that the category is thin, most of what's sold is small and low-powered, and the thing
that would actually improve the air in most cars costs less and is already fitted to the
vehicle.

So this is that article instead.

## The filter you already have

Nearly every car built since the early 2000s has a **cabin air filter** — a pleated
filter cleaning the air that enters through the ventilation system. It sits behind the
glovebox on most cars, sometimes under the dash or beneath the windshield cowl.

Most people have never replaced theirs.

A filter that's been in place for five years is loaded with dust, pollen, leaf debris and
whatever else the intake pulled in. That does two things: it restricts airflow, which is
why the fan seems weaker than it used to be, and it stops filtering effectively, which is
the actual air quality problem.

**Replacing it is cheap, usually takes a few minutes, and on many cars needs no tools** —
a couple of clips behind the glovebox. If you don't know when yours was last changed, it's
overdue.

## Why a purifier struggles in a car

The physics are unfavourable, and it's worth being clear about why.

An air purifier in a sealed room gets to work on the same air repeatedly, cleaning it a
little more each pass. **A car doesn't work like that.** While you're driving, the cabin
exchanges air with the outside continuously — through the ventilation system and around
door seals — so a small 12V unit is trying to clean air that's being replaced faster than
it can process it.

That's a much harder problem than the same device faces at home, and it's why a fresh
filter on the intake outperforms a purifier working on air that's already inside.

## If the complaint is a smell, a purifier is the wrong tool

This is the most common reason people buy one, and the most common reason they're
disappointed.

**Musty smell when the AC starts** is usually mould or bacteria on the evaporator, which
sits wet and dark behind the dashboard. That smell is generated *downstream* of any
purifier and blown directly at you. The fixes are a new cabin filter, an evaporator
cleaner sprayed into the intake, and running the fan without AC for the last few minutes
of a drive so the evaporator dries out.

**Persistent stale or sour smell** is usually contamination in the interior itself —
spilled drink in carpet, food under a seat, damp that never dried. A purifier masks
nothing here; the material is still there generating odour.

## A word on ozone generators

These get marketed for car deodorising, and they need a specific caution rather than a
recommendation.

**Ozone is a respiratory irritant.** Units sold for this purpose can produce
concentrations that aren't safe to breathe. If one is used at all, the car needs thorough
ventilation before anyone gets in — and the underlying source of the smell still needs
removing, because ozone treatment doesn't remove the material generating it.

## When a purifier does make sense

There are legitimate cases, and it's not our place to say nobody should buy one:

- **Significant allergies**, where every incremental reduction in pollen helps — though a
  higher-rated cabin filter is still the first move.
- **Heavy traffic commuting** in poor air quality, where you're recirculating rather than
  drawing outside air in.
- **You've already done the filter** and want a further improvement.

What we won't do is publish a ranked list of purifiers we couldn't verify. When we
researched this category, several listings we found were dead or turned out to be
replacement filters sold under a purifier's product name — which is its own signal about
how well served this category is.

## The order that actually works

1. **Replace the cabin air filter.** Cheap, quick, almost certainly overdue.
2. **Find what's actually producing the smell** — evaporator, spill, or damp.
3. **Vacuum and dry the interior properly.**
4. **Then**, if you still want one, consider a purifier — with realistic expectations.

Most people stop being bothered after step one, which is rather the point.
