Interior detailing rewards doing things in the right order more than it rewards
expensive products. Most of the frustration — cleaning the same surface twice, dust
settling on what you just finished — comes from sequence, not technique.

## The order

**1. Empty it.** Everything out — mats, bottles, the contents of door pockets. You cannot
clean around objects.

**2. Dry work first, top down.** Dust and vacuum starting at the headliner and working
downward. Gravity means everything you dislodge lands on surfaces below, so anything you
clean before the surfaces above it will need doing again.

**3. Compressed air or a soft brush into the gaps.** Vents, seat rails, seams, around the
gear selector. Blow it out onto the floor, then vacuum.

**4. Hard surfaces.** Dashboard, door cards, console, trim.

**5. Fabric or leather.** Seats and carpet.

**6. Interior glass, last.** Always last. Anything you do afterwards will mist or splash
it.

## The dry-dust rule

**Never wipe dry dust off a hard surface.** Blow it off, brush it off, or vacuum it —
then wipe.

This matters most on **piano-black trim**, which is the most scratch-prone surface in a
modern car. Wiping dry dust across gloss black plastic is the fastest way to fill it with
fine scratches, and it's essentially uncorrectable without polishing.

Same principle as the exterior: dry contact plus grit equals scratches.

## What to use on the dashboard

A damp low-pile microfibre towel and a mild interior cleaner covers almost everything.

What to avoid: **greasy silicone dressings**. They look impressive for a day, then:

- Create glare on the inside of the windscreen, which is a genuine visibility problem
- Attract dust, so the surface looks worse within a week
- Can go sticky in heat
- Build up unevenly over repeated applications

A matte, clean finish is safer to drive with and much easier to maintain.

## Screens and switchgear

Screens get a dedicated, clean, dry low-pile towel and nothing else — no cleaner sprayed
directly, ever. Most infotainment screens have anti-glare coatings that solvents damage
permanently.

For switchgear and buttons, a slightly damp towel wrapped over a fingertip gives you
control without pushing moisture into the electronics.

## Fabric and carpet

Vacuum thoroughly *before* introducing any moisture. Wet dirt becomes mud and works
deeper into the fibres.

For stains, work from the outside inward so you don't spread the edge, and blot rather
than scrub. Use as little liquid as will do the job — over-wetting carpet means moisture
trapped in the underlay, which is one of the most common causes of persistent damp smells.

## Smells

Air fresheners mask. Find the source instead:

- Damp carpet or underlay, often from a door seal or a spill
- Something under a seat
- **The cabin air filter**, which is cheap, frequently neglected, and responsible for a
  surprising share of stubborn interior odours

## Towels

Keep interior towels separate from paint towels and from glass towels. An interior towel
picks up dressings and cleaners that will streak glass immediately, and a towel that has
been on carpet carries grit you don't want anywhere near piano black.
