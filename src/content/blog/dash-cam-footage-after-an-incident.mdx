A dash cam is only useful if the footage survives the hour after the thing it recorded.
The default behaviour of every loop-recording camera works against that.

## The clock is already running

A dash cam records in a loop: it fills the card, then overwrites the oldest footage and
continues.

Unless the G-sensor triggered and locked the file, **the camera has no idea anything
happened.** It keeps looping, and depending on card size and channel count it can reach
the clip you need within hours.

That's why the first action matters more than the careful ones that follow.

## Stop the loop first

Press the **emergency lock button** if your camera has one — it moves the current clip into
a protected folder the loop won't touch. Most cameras trigger this automatically on a hard
impact, but not every incident is a hard impact.

If there's no lock button, **power the camera down.** Nothing recording means nothing
overwriting.

Worth knowing where that button is before you need it. Hunting for it afterwards costs
exactly the minutes that matter.

## Take the card out rather than using the app

Wi-fi transfer works and it's the least reliable option available. It's slow, large files
sometimes fail partway, and you're operating a phone app in a stressful situation.

Pull the card, copy from a reader. Use the app to confirm you've got the right clip if
that's convenient, but do the actual copying properly.

## Keep the original file untouched

Original files carry metadata — timestamps, and on GPS-equipped cameras speed and location
data. Re-encoding or editing can strip it.

If a shorter clip is useful for sharing, make that a copy and preserve the original
separately. It costs nothing and it's the version that matters if the footage is ever used
formally.

## Own a second card

This is the practical conclusion of everything above.

The card from an incident should come **out** of the camera and stay out. If you leave it
in, the camera keeps looping and may eventually overwrite even the protected folder once
it fills.

But a camera with no card isn't recording, and you still have to drive the car. A spare
card in the glovebox means you can preserve the original immediately without losing
coverage — which is why card capacity and having a second one are worth thinking about
before you need either. Both are covered in
[dash cam memory cards](/guides/dash-cam-memory-cards/).

## Check it's actually working, periodically

The worst version of this is discovering after an incident that the card failed silently
weeks ago and nothing has been recorded since.

Endurance cards wear out, and dash cams generally don't warn you. Pull a recent clip every
month or two and confirm it plays — that habit, and formatting the card in-camera
regularly, is what makes the rest of this article possible.
