"Tire shine rots your tires" is one of the more persistent claims in car care. Like most
persistent claims, it's a distorted version of something real.

## The tire protects itself, chemically

Rubber compounds include **antiozonants** — protective additives that slowly migrate to
the surface and shield it from ozone and UV, which are the actual causes of sidewall
cracking.

That migration is also why tires develop a brown film. Most people read that as dirt and
scrub it off. It's the tire's own protection arriving where it's meant to be.

## Where the real risk sits

**Not in the silicone. In the carrier.**

Silicone suspended in a water-based carrier is harmless to rubber. The same silicone in an
aggressive petroleum solvent is a different proposition, because the solvent can leach out
those antiozonants faster than the tire replenishes them from its finite internal supply.

Over years of repeated application, that's a plausible mechanism for accelerated browning
and, eventually, cracking. Over a season, on a car you wash monthly, it's a marginal
concern.

This is why "silicone-free" marketing tends to miss the point — it names the wrong
ingredient.

## Telling them apart

**Water-based:** water listed first, satin rather than mirror finish, washes off your
hands easily, low odor.

**Solvent-based:** strong petroleum smell, high-gloss wet look, stubborn on skin.

The glossiest "wet look" products are almost always solvent-carried. That's the tradeoff
being made — appearance for carrier aggression.

## Stop scrubbing the brown off aggressively

The instinct to attack browning with a strong degreaser every wash removes protection the
tire then has to rebuild from a limited reserve.

A pH-neutral cleaner, normal agitation, and a sensible wash cadence keep tires looking
right without stripping them. If you're using an acidic wheel cleaner nearby, keep it off
the rubber — the reasoning is in
[wheel cleaner pH](/blog/wheel-cleaner-ph-explained/).

## Keep it in proportion

Age, UV exposure, heat cycling, and chronic under-inflation cause vastly more sidewall
cracking than any dressing does. A water-based dressing used normally is not a meaningful
risk to your tires.
