The honest answer is more than you think, and the reason isn't fussiness — it's that a
towel which has stopped working doesn't just fail, it starts causing damage.

## The count, by job

For a normal maintenance wash on a mid-size car:

| Job | How many | GSM |
|---|---|---|
| Drying panels | 1–2 | 900–1200 |
| Glass (inside and out) | 2 | 300–400 |
| Interior surfaces | 1–2 | 350–500 |
| General purpose / door shuts | 2 | 350–500 |
| Wheels — dedicated, never reused | 1–2 | Any |
| **Total** | **7–10** | |

Add wax or sealant and you'll want another two or three plush towels for buffing, which
is where the professional figure of 10–15 comes from.

## Why running lean causes damage

Three specific failure modes, all of which happen when you don't have a clean towel to
hand:

**Saturation.** A drying towel that's holding all the water it can stops lifting and
starts pushing. That's when streaks and water spots appear — not because the towel is
bad, but because it's full.

**Contamination carry-over.** A towel that's picked up grit holds it in the pile. Every
subsequent panel gets that grit dragged across it. The two-bucket method exists to stop
this happening with the wash mitt; the same logic applies to towels.

**The dropped towel.** A towel hits the ground and picks up sharp grit. If it's your only
clean one, the temptation to rinse it and carry on is strong. It shouldn't be resisted
through willpower — it should be resisted by having another towel.

## The minimum viable kit

If you're starting from nothing and don't want to spend much:

1. **One good drying towel**, 900+ GSM twisted loop. Worth buying properly — see our
   [drying towel guide](/guides/best-car-drying-towels/).
2. **A six-pack of 300–500 GSM edgeless towels.** These cover glass, interior, door
   shuts and general work. Cheap in multipacks.
3. **Two towels marked for wheels only.** Can be the cheapest you can find. They will
   never touch paint again.

That's roughly nine towels for not much money, and it covers everything.

## Buy the cheap ones in bulk

The economics split neatly:

- **General-purpose 300–500 GSM towels**: buy in packs of six or twelve. You'll use them
  constantly, they're interchangeable, and per-towel cost drops sharply.
- **Drying towels**: buy individually and choose carefully. One well-made 900 GSM towel
  genuinely outperforms three mediocre ones, and this is the towel doing the
  highest-risk job.

## Keeping them straight

Colour-code by job and hold the line. One colour for glass, one for paint, one for
wheels. The colour does nothing on its own — the discipline does.

When a towel gets retired to dirty duty, snip a corner off it. It's a permanent mark that
survives laundering and stops a wheel towel quietly reappearing in the paint pile six
months later.

Whatever the count, [how you launder them](/blog/microfibre-care-101/) affects your
results more than how many you own.
