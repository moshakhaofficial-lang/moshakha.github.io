The first step of a good car wash is the one people rush, and it's **the one that protects
the paint**.

## What the rinse actually does

Dirt on a car isn't all the same. Some of it is stuck on, and some of it is **loose grit
sitting on top**, the sharp bits that scratch when you drag a mitt through them.

A thorough rinse lifts and flushes those particles away before your mitt touches the
surface. Less grit on the panel means less grit in the mitt, which means fewer fine
scratches. It's the simplest piece of the process behind
[avoiding swirl marks](/how-to/how-to-avoid-swirl-marks/).

## How to do it

1. **Start at the top** and work down, so dirt flows away from cleaner areas.
2. **Spend more time on the lower half**, where grit collects: sills, bumpers, behind the
   wheels.
3. **Keep going until the runoff looks mostly clear**, not just until the car is wet.
4. **Don't blast.** A pressure washer on a wide tip is fine; a hose is fine. See
   [pressure washer nozzle angles](/blog/pressure-washer-nozzle-angles-for-cars/) for
   which tip to use.

If you use a foam cannon, the pre-foam step does extra loosening before the rinse, but
it doesn't replace the wash itself, as
[snow foam vs shampoo](/blog/snow-foam-vs-car-shampoo/) explains.

## What a rinse won't do

**It won't remove traffic film**, the thin oily layer that makes a car look dull even
after the loose dirt is gone. That needs soap and gentle contact. The rinse is there to
make the contact wash safe.

## The rest of the chain

A pre-rinse is one piece of a safe routine:

- **Rinse** to remove loose grit.
- **Wash with a lubricated mitt**, rinsing the mitt often. The
  [two-bucket method](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method/) keeps the grit out of the soap.
- **Use a quality mitt** that pulls grit into its pile rather than pushing it around.
