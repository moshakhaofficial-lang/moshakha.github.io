A microfiber towel that used to soak up water fast and now beads it, or drags it around
without absorbing, hasn't worn out. In almost every case, it's coated in something.

## The actual cause: residue, not wear

Microfiber absorbs water through capillary action in microscopic gaps between the
strands. **Fabric softener is specifically designed to coat fibers** — that's how it
makes regular laundry feel soft — and that coating fills the same gaps that give
microfiber its absorbency. Detergent additives and dryer sheets do a version of the same
thing.

The fibers themselves are usually undamaged. They're just physically blocked from doing
what they're built to do.

## The fix: a strip wash

A strip wash is a wash cycle with **no detergent, no fabric softener, and no dryer
sheets** — hot water alone, or hot water with a small amount of plain dish soap
specifically without added softening agents or fragrance oils. The goal is removal, not
addition: you're washing residue off, not putting a different product on.

Run the towels through on hot, skip the softener dispenser entirely, and check
absorbency once dry. A towel that was genuinely just coated will noticeably improve.

## What doesn't work

**Regular laundry detergent** often contains its own additives, some of which
re-coat the fibers with a different residue rather than removing the original one.
**Fabric softener of any kind** is the specific thing causing the problem — using less of
it doesn't help, it needs to be absent entirely.

## Preventing it from happening again

Wash microfiber separately from regular laundry, skip the softener dispenser every time
— not just for microfiber loads if you share a machine — and avoid dryer sheets on
anything microfiber. The full washing and drying routine, including why heat matters, is
in [microfiber care basics](/blog/microfibre-care-101/).

## When it isn't residue

If a strip wash genuinely doesn't restore absorbency, the towel may actually be worn
out — thin, matted fibers from age and heavy use eventually do lose capacity, and no
amount of washing brings that back. At that point it's a
[replacement](/guides/best-microfiber-towels-for-cars/), not a cleaning problem.
