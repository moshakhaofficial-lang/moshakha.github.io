Short answer: **no, never.** Not in the wash, not as dryer sheets, not the "gentle" ones.

This single mistake is behind a large share of complaints about microfibre towels not
absorbing, leaving streaks, or "going bad" — and it is entirely reversible if you catch
it.

## What softener actually does

Fabric softener doesn't change the fibre. It **coats** it.

The softness you feel on a bath towel is a thin waxy film deposited on the surface of
every fibre, reducing friction so the fabric feels smoother against skin. On cotton
that's a reasonable trade.

Microfibre works completely differently. Its absorbency comes from **split fibres** —
each filament divided into many finer wedge-shaped strands, creating an enormous surface
area and thousands of capillary gaps that pull water in.

A waxy coating fills those gaps. The capillary action stops. The towel is now a piece of
smooth plastic fabric that pushes water around instead of drinking it.

## The symptoms

If you've used softener, you'll see some combination of:

- **Water beads on the towel** instead of soaking straight in
- **Streaking** on glass and paint — the waxy film transfers onto the surface
- **Reduced drying capacity** — the towel saturates almost immediately
- **A slick or slippery feel** that's different from plush softness

That streaking point is worth dwelling on: you're not just failing to remove water,
you're actively depositing softener residue onto the panel.

## Fixing towels you've already softened

Contamination is usually reversible. Do a **strip wash**:

1. Wash the towels **alone**, with **no detergent at all**.
2. Add about **one cup of plain white vinegar** to the wash cycle.
3. Run a **second full rinse cycle** with nothing added.
4. **Air dry.**

The vinegar's acidity dissolves the waxy film and any hard-water mineral deposits sitting
alongside it. Repeat once if the towel improves but isn't fully recovered.

Don't make this routine — vinegar is a restorative treatment, not a maintenance step.

## What a strip wash can't fix

**Heat damage.** Microfibre is polyester and polyamide, both plastics. A hot wash or a
hot dryer melts the split fibre tips and fuses them back together. A fused fibre has lost
the structure that made it work, permanently.

How to tell the difference: if a strip wash makes no difference and the pile feels stiff
or slightly crunchy rather than plush, that's heat, and the towel is finished. Retire it
to door shuts, wheel arches and garage spills.

## What to use instead

Plain liquid detergent, no softeners, no optical brighteners, no heavy fragrance — and
**less than the bottle says**. Excess detergent doesn't rinse out fully and becomes its
own residue problem.

Dedicated microfibre detergents exist and are good, but they aren't essential. What
matters is what you leave out.
