Bird droppings get treated as an annoyance to deal with at the next wash. On a hot panel
that's a decision with a cost.

## The damage is acid, not mess

Droppings are acidic, and as the water content evaporates the remaining acid
**concentrates**. At the same time, clear coat softens slightly in heat.

Those two things together are the mechanism. Concentrated acid on softened clear coat
bites into the surface rather than sitting on it, leaving a shallow etched impression of
the dropping's own outline.

That's why the mark often survives long after the mess itself has been washed away. You're
not looking at residue — you're looking at a shape cut into the paint.

## The timeline is shorter than people think

**Same day** — wipes off with a damp towel, no trace.

**A few days in warm weather** — a dulled outline where the clear coat has started to
etch. Often polishes out.

**A week in hot sun** — a permanent etched ring that only levelling the surrounding clear
coat will remove, if it can be removed at all.

In cool overcast weather the same dropping might sit for a week harmlessly. **Heat is the
variable**, which is why summer plus parking under trees is the combination that costs
people paint.

## Soften, never scrub

A dried dropping contains grit and seed fragments. Wiping it across paint dry is
genuinely abrasive — you'll add scratches to the etching you were already going to have.

Lay a microfiber soaked in water or quick detailer over it for several minutes, then lift
straight off with almost no pressure. Re-soak and repeat rather than pressing harder.

## Protection buys time, not immunity

A wax or coating gives the acid a sacrificial layer to work through first. On a dropping
removed reasonably promptly, that can be the difference between a mark and no mark.

It does not make a dropping left through a hot week harmless. Prompt removal is the actual
defence; protection just widens the window.

## If the mark is already there

Run a fingernail over it. Smooth means residue — keep working at it gently. A ring you can
**feel** means it's etched.

Light etching generally polishes out. That's the same distinction, and the same fix, as
etched water spots — covered in
[removing water spots](/how-to/how-to-remove-water-spots/).

## The practical habit

This is the argument for contamination-driven washing rather than calendar-driven washing,
which is the whole point of

Droppings and sap don't wait for your schedule. Everything else mostly does.
