A wash mitt doesn't announce when it's stopped being safe. It looks broadly the same on
the day it starts scratching paint as it did the week before.

## The non-negotiable one: it hit the ground

If a mitt lands on the driveway, it's finished for paint work.

Driveway grit is coarse, sharp, and it embeds into the pile rather than sitting on it.
Rinsing won't reliably remove it, and — this is the important part — **you have no way to
verify whether it's gone.** The next panel you wash is the test, and failing it means fine
scratches across an entire door.

You don't have to bin it. Demote it to wheels, arches, or door jambs, where its remaining
life is genuinely useful and the stakes are low.

## The pile has matted or flattened

A mitt works by holding grit **away** from the paint, suspended in deep pile, while the
lubricating film does the rest. Matted pile can't do that. The grit sits at the surface,
in contact with the clear coat, exactly where you don't want it.

Compare a used mitt against a new one and the difference is usually obvious the moment you
squeeze both.

## It feels rough or grabby when wet

Trust this one — if you can feel roughness, the paint is feeling it too.

The first thing to try is a strip wash: hot water, no detergent, no softener. Roughness is
often residue coating the fibers rather than genuine wear, and residue is fixable. The
method is in
[restoring microfiber absorbency](/blog/how-to-restore-microfiber-absorbency/).

If it's still rough after that, the pile has matted or picked up embedded contamination,
and it isn't recoverable.

## Wash count is the wrong metric

People want a number, and there isn't a useful one. A mitt used on two lightly dusty
garage-kept cars is in different shape after fifty washes than one used on a work truck
after ten.

Judge by condition — full, soft, lofted pile means it's still working.

## Laundering decides most of the lifespan

More mitts are killed by the washing machine than by use:

- **Never with regular laundry.** Cotton lint embeds in the pile permanently.
- **Never with fabric softener or dryer sheets.** They coat the fibers, which is the exact
  mechanism that destroys absorbency.
- **Hot wash, low or no heat drying.** High dryer heat can melt fine microfiber strands.

The full care routine is in [microfiber care basics](/blog/microfibre-care-101/).

## Keep a retirement tier

The practical system: paint mitts get retired to wheels, wheel mitts get retired to
arches and jambs, and arch mitts get thrown away. Nothing moves back up the ladder, ever.

That way a compromised mitt always has somewhere useful to go, and paint never sees one
that's been anywhere it shouldn't. Construction differences that affect how long each
tier lasts are covered in [best car wash mitts](/guides/best-car-wash-mitts/).
