Dealership badges, old parking permits, previous owners' decals. The removal is simple and
the two common mistakes are both avoidable.

## Heat does the work

Cold vinyl tears into fragments and leaves the adhesive behind. Warm vinyl lifts in one
piece and brings most of the adhesive with it.

A hairdryer on a moderate setting is enough, and it's safer than a heat gun — a heat gun
works faster but concentrates far more heat, and overheating paint or distorting nearby
plastic trim is easy to do.

Aim for the vinyl feeling **warm and pliable**, not hot to the touch.

## Pull back over itself, not away

Lift a corner and pull it back across the panel at a shallow angle, so the vinyl folds
back on itself as it releases.

Pulling straight up puts tension on a small area and tears it. The shallow angle spreads
the load along the bond line and is the difference between one piece and thirty.

Work slowly. If it starts tearing, stop and apply more heat rather than more force.

## No blades on paint

Plastic razor blades are fine on glass. On a painted panel the risk-to-benefit is simply
wrong — a solvent and patience achieves the same thing without the possibility of a
permanent gouge.

For the residue: a dedicated adhesive remover, applied and given **dwell time**, then
worked with a cloth. Isopropyl alcohol handles light residue.

## The ghost outline isn't damage

This catches people out and reads as a mistake.

Paint under a long-fitted decal has been **shielded from UV** while everything around it
weathered slightly. So once the sticker's gone you can see its outline as a difference in
tone.

That's weathering, not harm. A polish over the surrounding area helps blend it, and it
reduces over time as the exposed patch catches up. On an older car it can be quite
pronounced and worth expecting before you start.

## Reprotect the area afterwards

The paint under a decal has had no wax or sealant on it for as long as the decal was
there, and the solvent you just used stripped whatever was around the edges.

Wash the panel, then reapply protection to that area. Skipping it leaves a bare patch in
the middle of a protected panel, which contaminates faster than everything around it.
