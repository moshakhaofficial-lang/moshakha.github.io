Magnetic phone mounts are popular because they're quick: drop the phone on, and it stays.
The catch is that the things people like about them all pile heat onto a device that
doesn't enjoy it.

## Where the heat comes from

Three sources stack up:

1. **Sunlight** on a dashboard or windscreen, which can be intense.
2. **Wireless charging**, which is less efficient than a cable and sheds the difference as
   warmth.
3. **The phone's own workload**, such as navigation, screen on, and mobile data.

Any one is manageable. **All three together is what triggers the "temperature too high"
warning.**

## What the phone does about it

Modern phones protect themselves. They slow charging, dim the screen, and eventually pause
charging or show a warning. So it isn't usually dangerous, but it's irritating, because
charging stops at precisely the moment navigation is draining the battery.

## Placement helps most

- **Keep it out of direct sun** where possible. A mount low on the dash or lower on the
  windscreen can help.
- **Use airflow.** In summer, a vent mount puts cool air across the phone. In winter the
  vent blows warm air on it, so you may prefer a different spot.
- **Check sightlines before anything else.** Our piece on
  [phone mount placement](/blog/phone-mount-placement-and-sightlines/) explains why the
  view through the glass comes first.

## Watch the case

The magnet has to reach through the case. **A thick rugged case cuts the holding force**,
and metal in a case can interfere. If the phone sags or slides on bumps, a MagSafe-rated
case is the usual fix.
