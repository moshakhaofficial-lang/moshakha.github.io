Pollen season produces more fine scratching than any other time of year, and almost all of
it comes from a single reflex: the car looks dusty, so people wipe it.

## Why dry-wiping pollen is the problem

Pollen grains aren't smooth. Many are textured or spiked — that's how they cling to
insects — and some become mildly acidic when wet.

Dragging that across clear coat with a dry cloth is the same mechanism that causes swirl
marks during washing: abrasive particles, no lubrication, pressure from your hand. The
difference is that people do it casually, in a car park, without thinking of it as
"washing" at all.

One pass doesn't do visible harm. A whole spring of them does.

## Rinse frequently instead

The useful change in pollen season isn't washing more thoroughly — it's **rinsing more
often.**

A plain hose-down removes the bulk of the pollen with zero contact risk. No mitt, no
bucket, no technique required. Do that every few days and the pollen never accumulates
into something you feel tempted to wipe.

Save the full contact wash for when there's genuine road film underneath, and pre-rinse
heavily before touching anything — the reasoning is in
[how to avoid swirl marks](/how-to/how-to-avoid-swirl-marks/).

When you do wash properly, lubrication matters more than usual because of what's sitting
on the paint.

## If you can't rinse — use a real waterless wash

Apartment parking, no hose, pollen everywhere. This is the legitimate case for a waterless
wash product, which carries enough lubricant and polymer content to encapsulate grains and
let the towel slide rather than drag.

**Not a quick detailer.** That's the distinction that matters here, and getting it wrong
is how careful people scratch their own paint — covered in
[quick detailer: the dry-panel warning](/blog/quick-detailer-when-to-use/) and
[the waterless wash guide](/blog/waterless-wash-guide/).

## The part that affects you more than the car

If you have hay fever, the cabin air filter matters more than the paint does.

Spring is when filters load fastest, and a clogged filter both reduces airflow and stops
doing the filtering you want. Replacing it at the **start** of pollen season rather than
the end is ten minutes that changes how the car feels to sit in —
[how to replace a cabin air filter](/how-to/how-to-replace-cabin-air-filter/).

Also worth avoiding recirculate mode with a loaded filter: you're circulating cabin air
through a filter that's already full rather than drawing filtered fresh air in.

## Does wax help?

It helps pollen **release** during a rinse, which is a real benefit when your strategy is
frequent rinsing.

It doesn't stop pollen landing, and it doesn't make dry-wiping safe. Treat it as something
that makes the rinsing approach work better, not as a substitute for it.
