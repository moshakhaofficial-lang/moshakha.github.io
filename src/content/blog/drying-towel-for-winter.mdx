Winter drying advice tends to focus on getting the paint dry fast so water spots don't
form. That's a summer problem wearing a winter coat. In freezing conditions, the water
that actually causes trouble isn't on your paint at all.

## The paint was never the problem

Flat panels shed most of their water and evaporate the rest reasonably quickly, even in
the cold. Nobody's morning is ruined by a slightly damp hood.

**Water trapped in door and boot seals is a different matter.** A rubber seal holds a
thin film of water in a shaded, enclosed channel, where it sits until the temperature
drops and freezes — bonding the seal to the door frame. The result is a door that either
won't open, or opens and tears the seal.

The same applies to locks, handles, mirror housings, and the gaps behind badges: small
trapped volumes, sheltered from airflow, that freeze first and fastest.

## Dry in this order when it's cold

**Seals first.** Open each door and wipe the rubber seal and the frame it presses
against. This is the single highest-value minute of a winter dry.

**Then handles, locks and mirror housings.** Small volumes, quick to do, and the ones
that strand you at the worst moment.

**Then the panels.** They were going to be fine anyway, but a fast pass with a
high-capacity towel gets you out of the cold sooner — which matters more than usual when
water is actively freezing as you work.

## Why finishing fast matters more in winter

In mild weather, taking your time drying is harmless. Below freezing, every minute the
car stands wet is a minute water is migrating into gaps and starting to freeze. A large,
high-capacity towel that covers panels in fewer passes isn't about luxury here — it
shortens the window in which things can freeze. Sizing and capacity are covered in
[best car drying towels](/guides/best-car-drying-towels/).

## Forced air genuinely helps here

This is the one case where a leaf blower or dedicated air dryer earns its keep for
reasons beyond convenience. Air reaches into seals, lock barrels, badge edges and mirror
housings — exactly the places a towel physically cannot reach, and exactly where winter
failures originate.

## And drive it briefly afterward

A short drive after washing warms the car, moves air through the panel gaps, and helps
clear water a towel never reached. It's a small habit that prevents the frozen-door
morning more reliably than a more thorough drying session in the driveway.
