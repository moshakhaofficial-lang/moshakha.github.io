500 GSM and 800 GSM are the two weights you'll meet most often, and the choice between
them is where most people first run into the "higher must be better" assumption.

It isn't. These are **different tools**, and the more expensive one is the wrong answer
for several common jobs.

## The short answer

| | **500 GSM** | **800 GSM** |
|---|---|---|
| Pile | Medium-low | Medium-high, plush |
| Water capacity | Moderate | High |
| Surface contact | Even, precise | Cushioned, less precise |
| Best at | Interiors, door shuts, general wiping, product application | Wax and sealant removal, quick detailer, light drying |
| Poor at | Drying a whole car | Interiors, trim, anything tight |
| Dries after washing | Quickly | Slowly |
| Buy first? | **Yes** | Only as a second towel |

## Where 500 GSM wins

**Interiors and trim.** A shorter pile makes flatter, more even contact with hard
surfaces. On dashboards, door cards, screens and switchgear, that means it cleans
rather than skating over the texture. An 800 GSM towel is clumsy here — the deep pile
cushions the contact and carries grit into panel gaps.

**Anything needing precision.** Badges, vents, door shuts, around emblems. Lower pile
means you can feel the surface through the towel.

**General-purpose use.** If you're buying a stack of towels for miscellaneous work, 500
GSM is the weight to buy. It dries quickly after laundering, stores flat, and is cheap
enough to retire without hesitation.

**Applying product.** A shorter pile absorbs less of your wax or dressing, so more of it
ends up on the car rather than soaked into the towel.

## Where 800 GSM wins

**Removing wax and sealant.** This is the job 800 GSM is genuinely made for. The deeper
pile lifts and holds cured residue away from the paint instead of pushing it around, and
there's enough capacity to buff a whole panel without loading up.

**Quick detailer and spray wax.** Plush pile absorbs the excess and leaves a cleaner
finish than a thinner towel.

**Light drying on a small car.** It will work on a hatchback. It is not a drying towel,
and you will be wringing it out — but in a pinch, it's serviceable.

**Soft or delicate paint.** The extra cushioning gives a margin of safety on soft clear
coats where you'd rather not press.

## Where both are the wrong choice

**Drying a car properly.** Both sit below the practical drying range. You want 900–1200
GSM, and more importantly you want **twisted loop** rather than plush — the weave matters
more than the weight here. Our
[drying towel guide](/guides/best-car-drying-towels) covers what to buy instead.

**Glass.** Both are too plush. Deep pile cannot make consistent contact with a perfectly
smooth surface, so it smears the last film of moisture into streaks. Glass wants 300–400
GSM. This is the single most common cause of streaky windows, and no change of glass
cleaner will fix it.

## Which to buy first

**Buy 500 GSM first**, in a multipack, edgeless. It covers more jobs, it's cheaper, and
it launders better. Add 800 GSM later specifically for wax and sealant removal, when you
have a reason to.

The upgrade that will actually change your results isn't going from 500 to 800. It's
adding a **proper drying towel** at 900+ GSM in twisted loop, and a **low-pile glass
towel** at 300–400 GSM. Those two fill the gaps that neither weight here covers.

## Before you spend anything

GSM is only one of four specs that matter, and it's the least informative of them —
blend ratio, weave and edge construction all affect performance more. Our full
[breakdown of what microfibre GSM actually means](/guides/microfiber-gsm-explained)
covers how to read a listing properly.

And whichever you buy, [how you launder it](/blog/microfibre-care-101) will affect its
performance more than the 300 GSM difference discussed on this page.
