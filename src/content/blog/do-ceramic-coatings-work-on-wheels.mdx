Ceramic coatings get sold on paint. The gloss photos are all paint, the marketing is all
paint, and paint is where most people spend the money. Wheels are where the same product
saves more work.

## The return depends on the baseline

Coating paint takes something that was already fairly easy to wash and makes it a bit
easier, plus adds gloss. That's a real benefit, but it's an improvement on a task that
wasn't the problem.

**Wheels start from a much worse baseline.** Brake dust doesn't just land on a wheel — it
lands hot, and hot metal particles embed into the finish and then corrode in place.
That's why wheels need a dedicated cleaner, a brush, and the most physical effort of any
part of a normal wash.

Coat that surface, and the dust largely stops bonding. It sits on top and rinses away.
The hardest job on the car becomes one of the easiest, which is a bigger change than
anything a coating does for a hood.

## What a coating does and doesn't change

**It doesn't reduce brake dust.** The same amount lands on the wheel either way.

**It changes whether that dust bonds.** That distinction is the entire value proposition,
and it's the one most product pages blur.

## Decontaminate first, or you're sealing in the problem

A coating seals whatever is beneath it. Wheels that already have embedded iron particles
need those dissolved and removed before coating — otherwise the particles keep corroding
underneath a layer you can't get through.

That means a proper decontamination pass, including an iron remover, before any coating
goes near the wheel. The mechanism behind that purple bleed is explained in
[iron fallout remover](/blog/iron-fallout-remover-explained/).

## Take the wheels off if you're serious

The barrel and the inner face collect the most brake dust and are nearly impossible to
prep and coat properly while the wheel is on the car. Coating only the outer face still
helps, but it leaves the dirtiest area — where most of the benefit was — untreated.

## Expect shorter durability than on paint

Wheels heat-cycle every time you brake, take constant impact from road debris, and get
cleaned more aggressively than paint ever does. A coating's stated durability is measured
on paint under gentler conditions. On wheels, treat it as an optimistic ceiling, and
expect the barrel to wear before the face.

None of that changes the conclusion. Even at reduced durability, removing the worst job
from every wash is a better return than adding gloss to a panel that was already easy.
