"Bigger dries faster" is intuitive, and true up to a point. Past that point, size starts
working against you in ways that aren't obvious until you're standing there with a
soaking twelve-hundred-gram sheet.

## Where a large towel genuinely wins

**Roofs and bonnets.** Drape it flat and pull it across in one motion. On a large flat
surface this is fast and puts almost no pressure on the paint.

**Large vehicles.** On an SUV, van or truck, a small towel means constant wringing — and
a wrung towel never works as well as a dry one.

**Drying more than one car.** More capacity means fewer interruptions.

## Where it works against you

**Saturation is all-or-nothing.** This is the big one. A large towel soaks up as a single
unit. When it's full, the *entire towel* is full — there's no dry section to switch to.
Two medium towels give you a second dry one; one large towel gives you a wringing job.

**Wringing is genuinely hard.** A saturated 60x90cm high-GSM towel holds a lot of water
and takes real effort to wring properly. Most people don't get it fully wrung, which
means they continue with a towel that's still half-loaded.

**Control around detail areas.** Mirrors, badges, door handles, spoilers. A large towel
is unwieldy exactly where you need precision, and the temptation is to bunch it up —
which concentrates pressure into a small area, which is how marring happens.

**Drying and storage.** Large heavy towels take a long time to air-dry and need real
hanging space. Damp storage means mildew, and mildew permanently affects absorbency.

## The sizes that make sense

| Vehicle | Towel size | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hatchback, small sedan | ~40x60cm (16x24in) | One-handed control, no wasted capacity |
| Mid-size sedan, small SUV | ~50x70cm (20x28in) | Panel in one pass, still manageable |
| Large SUV, truck, van | ~60x90cm (24x36in) | Reach and capacity genuinely needed |

## The technique that matters more than size

However large your towel:

- **Lay it flat and pull.** Don't rub, and don't work in circles.
- **Let it do the work.** No pressure. A good drying towel needs contact, not force.
- **Start at the top.** Roof, glass, bonnet, then downward.
- **Use a separate low-pile towel for glass.** No drying towel of any size will leave
  glass streak-free.
- **Drop it, retire it.** A towel that's touched the ground carries grit.

## The recommendation

For most people: **two medium towels rather than one very large one.** You get a dry
towel to switch to, easier wringing, faster laundering, and a spare if one hits the
ground.
