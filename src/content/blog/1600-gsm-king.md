Search for drying towels and you'll find 1600 GSM sold as the top of the range — the
serious enthusiast's choice, the "king". It's the biggest number on the shelf, so it must
be the best one.

It depends entirely on what you're drying.

## Where 1600 GSM genuinely earns it

There are real cases for this much towel:

- **Large SUVs, trucks and vans.** Enough capacity to dry the whole vehicle without
  stopping to wring out.
- **Two cars back to back.** A 1600 GSM towel will still be working when a 900 GSM one
  has given up.
- **Very wet climates**, or if you're drying after rain rather than after a wash.

If any of those describe you, this is a legitimate purchase and not a marketing trick.

## Where it's the wrong tool

**Most cars.** On a sedan or hatchback you will never approach the capacity limit of a
1600 GSM towel. What you will notice is the weight when it's loaded, the bulk around
mirrors and badges, and how long it takes to air-dry.

**Glass.** Always, without exception. The deeper the pile, the worse it makes contact
with a smooth surface, and the more it smears rather than lifts. Glass wants 300–400 GSM.

**Interiors.** Far too clumsy for trim, screens and switchgear, and the deep pile carries
grit into panel gaps.

**Anywhere you need to feel the surface.** Badges, vents, door shuts. A thick towel
insulates your hand from what you're doing.

## The storage problem nobody mentions

A 1600 GSM towel holds an enormous amount of water and takes a long time to release it.
In practice that means a full day hung somewhere with real airflow.

Stored damp — folded in a boot, or in a bucket — microfibre develops mildew. That's not
just a smell problem: it permanently affects absorbency, and washing doesn't reliably fix
it.

If you don't have somewhere to hang a large heavy towel properly, that's a genuine
argument for something lighter.

## Why the number gets pushed so hard

GSM is a single figure that looks like a quality score and is trivial to put on a
listing. It's a good marketing asset.

The more useful specs — blend ratio, weave type, edge construction — are harder to
advertise and harder to compare at a glance. And crucially, a retailer selling premium
high-GSM towels has no commercial reason to publish the sentence "400 GSM is the right
choice for your windows".

That's not a conspiracy, just incentives. But it does mean the loudest spec is the least
informative one. Our [GSM guide](/guides/microfiber-gsm-explained) covers what to look at
instead.

## The honest recommendation

**Buy 1600 GSM if** you drive something large, or you dry more than one vehicle at a
time.

**Otherwise buy 900–1200 GSM twisted loop**, and spend the difference on a low-pile glass
towel — which will improve your results far more than the extra 400 GSM would. The
[drying towel guide](/guides/best-car-drying-towels) has specific picks for both cases.
