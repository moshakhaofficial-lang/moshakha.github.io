Drying gets treated as the boring part of the wash — the bit after the real work. It's
actually the step most likely to put marks in your paint, and the one where those marks
show up most clearly.

## Why drying is the risky step

By the time you're drying, the paint is clean and the lubrication is gone. The shampoo
that was keeping grit floating has been rinsed away.

Anything left on the panel — a particle that survived the wash, dust that settled while
you rinsed the other side — is now sitting on bare wet paint with a towel about to be
pressed onto it and dragged.

That's the same mechanism as washing without lubrication, and it happens at the end, on a
car you've just cleaned, where any marring is immediately visible.

## What a drying aid does

It restores the lubricating film for the final contact step.

Spray it onto the wet panel, and the towel glides instead of dragging. Grit slides
rather than grinding. Most aids also leave a small amount of gloss and short-term
protection behind, which is a genuine bonus but not the reason to use one.

The cost is about ten seconds per panel.

## Use enough, and use a clean towel

Both failure modes here are self-inflicted:

**Too little product** — the film isn't continuous, so you're back to dragging in patches.

**A contaminated towel** — one previously used for wax or sealant will streak no matter
how much aid you spray. Sort drying towels by job and
[launder them by category, without fabric softener](/blog/microfibre-care-101/), which is
the other common reason a towel smears rather than absorbs.

Spray a section, dry that section, move on. Letting the aid dry on the panel before you
wipe is the third way to get streaks.

## Quick detailer works, at the right dilution

Most people use a quick detailer as their drying aid, and that's entirely legitimate —
plenty of them list the use on the label.

One thing to note: because the panel is already wet, the product spreads much further
than it does on a dry car, so you need less than instinct suggests. That's the opposite
of normal quick detailer use, where under-applying is the classic mistake — the dry-panel
warning is in
[quick detailer: the dry-panel warning](/blog/quick-detailer-when-to-use/).

## On a coated car

Less necessary, because water sheets off and there's far less left to dry. Still useful
for lubrication on the remainder, and coating-specific hydrophobic sprays will top up the
water behavior.

Avoid heavy wax-based aids on a coated car — they can sit on top and mask the coating's
own hydrophobic performance, which tends to get misread as the coating failing.

## It doesn't fix water spots

A drying aid helps you dry faster and more completely, which is the real prevention for
water spots. It doesn't dissolve mineral deposits that have already formed — that needs a
dedicated remover, and etched spots need polishing. The distinction is in
[removing water spots](/how-to/how-to-remove-water-spots/).
