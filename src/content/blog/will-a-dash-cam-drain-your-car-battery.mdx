Short answer: **a dash cam won't drain your battery if it's set up correctly, and it can if
it isn't.** The deciding factor is where it gets power from.

## Three ways to power a dash cam

**1. The 12V socket, with power that cuts off with the ignition.** The camera only runs
when the car does. No drain when parked. This is the simplest setup, but it depends on your
car's socket behaviour, which we cover in
[does the 12V socket stay on with the ignition off](/blog/does-12v-socket-stay-on-with-ignition-off/).

**2. The 12V socket, with power that stays on.** On some cars the socket is live all the
time. The camera runs constantly, and with no cut-off it will draw the battery down.

**3. Hardwired to the fuse box.** The camera is wired to fuses, which lets it record while
parked. **This is where a cut-off matters.**

## How much does it use?

A dash cam is a small load next to things like headlights, but it's continuous. Over days
on a car that isn't driven, a small constant draw adds up to a flat battery. That's why the
cut-off isn't optional with parking mode.

## What a hardwire kit should have

- **A voltage cut-off** that stops the draw before the battery is too low to start the car.
- **An adjustable threshold**, so you can be conservative.
- **The right fuse taps**, which are listed in your owner's manual or fuse diagram.

Most dash cams with parking mode need a hardwire kit that's sold separately, so check
what's included before you buy. Our [installation guide](/how-to/how-to-install-a-dash-cam/)
walks through the fuse box and cable routing.

## Who should skip parking mode

- **Short-trip, rarely driven cars**, where the battery never gets a proper charge.
- **Cars with older batteries.** See [when to replace a car battery](/blog/car-battery-when-to-replace/).
- **Anyone uncomfortable working with a fuse box**, in which case get it installed.

The rest of the picture, including why resolution is oversold and what actually matters,
is in [our dash cam guide](/guides/best-dash-cams/).

## Verdict

For most drivers, a cam that powers off with the ignition does the job with zero battery
risk. **Add parking mode deliberately, with a voltage cut-off, or not at all.**
