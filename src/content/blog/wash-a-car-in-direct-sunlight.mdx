Yes, you can. You just shouldn't if you have a choice, and the reason isn't the one most
people give.

The sun isn't harming your paint. **The heat it puts into the panel is what causes the
trouble.**

## What actually goes wrong

A hot panel evaporates water quickly. That has two consequences:

- **Soap dries before you rinse it.** What's left is dried surfactant, a hazy film that
  looks like streaking and takes a second wash to shift.
- **Water spots form.** Tap water carries dissolved minerals. When the water flashes off,
  the minerals stay behind as spots. Fresh ones wipe away; ones baked on need more effort,
  which is covered in [removing water spots](/how-to/how-to-remove-water-spots/).

A hot panel also shortens your working time. Soap that should sit and lubricate is
drying while you're still washing the next section, which means more friction and more risk of
marring.

## The hand test

You don't need a thermometer. **Rest the back of your hand on the roof.** If it's
uncomfortable, it's too hot. Dark paint gets there much sooner than light paint, which is
one reason [washing a black car](/blog/how-to-wash-a-black-car/) is less forgiving.

## If you can only wash in the sun

Sometimes it's the only slot you have. These make it workable:

1. **Wash one panel at a time**, and rinse it straight away rather than soaping the whole
   car first.
2. **Start with the roof and work down**, since the roof is the hottest surface.
3. **Keep the panel wet.** Pre-wet each section before you touch it so the soap never meets
   a dry, hot surface.
4. **Use a soap with good lubricity** and mix it to the label. A slick wash solution buys you
   extra working time.
5. **Dry immediately** with a large, soft towel while the panel is still wet.

A proper [two-bucket method](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method/) helps here too, because
you're not wasting time going back for fresh soap.

## Shade, a garage, or an overcast day

The easy answer is also the best one. **A shaded driveway, a garage, or a cloudy day**
removes the whole problem. If you wash often, choosing your time slot is the single
cheapest upgrade to your routine.

For picking a soap to go with it, [our car wash soap guide](/guides/best-car-wash-soap/)
covers what pH-neutral means and which products are worth the money.

## The short version

Sun itself is fine. **Hot panels are not.** Test with your hand, work one panel at a time,
keep everything wet, and dry straight away.
