"Every two weeks" is the answer you'll find in most car care advice, and it's a
reasonable default for exactly one situation: a garage-kept car, mild climate, average
road conditions. Outside that description, it's mostly wrong, and it's worth
understanding why so you can work out your own interval instead of following a generic
one.

## The real variables

**Where the car sleeps.** A garage-kept car accumulates far less airborne dust,
pollen, tree sap, and bird activity than one parked under trees or on the street. This
alone can be the difference between a two-week and a one-week interval.

**Climate and coastal proximity.** Salt air near the coast accelerates corrosion on
unwashed paint and trim. Areas with heavy pollen seasons or frequent dust need more
frequent attention during those specific months, not year-round.

**Road salt in winter.** This is the biggest seasonal swing. Salted roads leave a
corrosive film on the underbody and wheel wells that plain rain doesn't fully clear.
Regions that salt roads heavily warrant a noticeably shorter interval — sometimes
weekly — during winter months specifically.

**What's actually landed on the car.** Bird droppings and tree sap are acidic and can
etch clear coat if left for days rather than hours. These aren't "wait for the next
scheduled wash" situations — they're a rinse-it-now situation regardless of where you
are in your normal cycle.

## Contamination-based, not calendar-based

The more useful mental model isn't "every N days" — it's "how long can this specific
contaminant safely sit before it stops being cosmetic and starts being a bonding or
etching risk." Dust is low-urgency. Bird droppings and sap are high-urgency. Road salt in
winter is high-urgency and recurring. A fixed calendar interval averages all of that into
one number that fits none of the individual cases well.

## Does frequent washing damage paint?

Not from frequency itself — from technique. A wash using a
[pH-neutral soap](/guides/best-car-wash-soap/), clean mitts, and
[the two-bucket method](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method/) is safe as often as it's
needed. What actually causes swirl marks and fine scratches is dirty tools and rushed
technique repeated over time, not the number of washes themselves — see
[avoiding swirl marks](/how-to/how-to-avoid-swirl-marks/) for the specific technique
mistakes that matter more than frequency.

## The practical answer

Set a baseline of every two to three weeks for a typically garage-kept car in a mild
climate, then override it immediately for anything acidic (bird droppings, sap) or
seasonal (winter road salt, heavy pollen). The baseline is a starting point, not a rule —
the contamination on the car is the actual signal.
