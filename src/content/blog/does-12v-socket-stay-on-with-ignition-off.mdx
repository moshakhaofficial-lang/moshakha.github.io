This question shows up constantly in reviews of corded 12V accessories — inflators,
vacuums, coolers — as a one-star complaint that reads like a product defect. It usually
isn't. It's a wiring decision the manufacturer made, and it varies by car in ways that
aren't obvious from the outside.

## Why manufacturers do this at all

A 12V socket that stays powered indefinitely is a battery drain waiting to happen. Leave
a phone charger plugged in and forget about it for a few days, and a socket with no
cutoff will flatten the battery. Cutting power when the ignition is off is a deliberate
protection feature — the tradeoff is that anything you own that depends on the socket
being live simply stops working the moment you take the key out.

**This is exactly the failure mode behind most one-star reviews of corded car
accessories.** The product works fine. The car's wiring just doesn't support the way the
buyer expected to use it.

## It isn't consistent — even within one car

The frustrating part is that this isn't a simple "newer cars do this, older cars don't"
rule. Some manufacturers wire the front console socket to stay live while a rear-seat
socket cuts out, or the reverse. Trim level can matter too. **Guessing from a similar car
you've owned before isn't reliable enough to buy equipment around.**

## How to actually test yours

Skip the guessing. With the engine off and the key out of the ignition (or, for
push-button start cars, with the car fully powered down rather than just in accessory
mode), plug something with a visible indicator light into the socket you intend to use.
If it lights up, that socket stays live. If nothing happens, it cuts with the ignition.

Test the **specific socket** you plan to use the accessory from, not just "a socket in
this car" — front and rear can genuinely differ.
