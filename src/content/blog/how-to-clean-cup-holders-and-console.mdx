The centre console is the dirtiest part of most interiors and the part that responds worst
to the usual approach, because the grime here isn't dust — it's sugar.

## Sticky changes the method

Dust sits on a surface and lifts off. Dried soft drink, coffee and melted sweets are
**bonded** to it.

Scrubbing at bonded residue dry does two things: it polishes the residue rather than
removing it, and it grinds whatever grit is mixed in against the plastic. That's how
console trim picks up fine scratching.

The technique that works is the same one used on bugs and sap: **soften first, wipe with
almost no pressure.**

## Check whether the liners come out

Many cars have a rubber or plastic insert in the cup holder well that lifts straight out.

Worth checking before you start, because a liner you can wash in a sink comes up far
better than one you're reaching into at arm's length. Some pull out with firm even
pressure, some have a small tab. If it doesn't move with moderate effort, it isn't
designed to.

## Soften, then wipe

Lay a damp cloth over dried residue for a minute, or hold a damp swab against it. It
releases with almost no effort once rehydrated.

This is a minute of waiting that replaces five minutes of scrubbing and the marring that
comes with it.

## The seams need a brush

Around the base of the gear selector, the handbrake surround, and the joins between trim
panels — a cloth bridges straight across these and never touches what's in them.

Work the brush while the area is still slightly damp, with a vacuum crevice tool held
nearby to catch what comes loose. Brushing dry just relocates crumbs into the gaps you're
trying to clear.

Don't spray cleaner directly into those gaps — liquid down beside a gear selector has
nowhere to evaporate to.

## Match the product to the surface

Diluted all-purpose cleaner is fine in a hard-plastic cup holder well.

It is **not** fine on the surrounding console trim, particularly soft-touch surfaces and
anything with a screen. That's an interior detailer job — the boundaries are in
[interior detailer vs all-purpose cleaner](/blog/interior-detailer-vs-all-purpose-cleaner/).

## Piano black is the fragile one

Treat gloss black trim as the most delicate surface in the car. It scratches from almost
nothing, and dried residue plus pressure is precisely how it gets marred.

Soften thoroughly, wipe with a clean plush microfiber in one direction, and never use a
paper towel or a brush on it. Marks in that finish are permanent and extremely visible —
which is why it's worth handling separately rather than as part of a general console wipe.

## Where it fits in the interior

Console and cup holders come **after** the vents and dashboard, because they're below them
and catch whatever falls. The full top-down sequence is in
