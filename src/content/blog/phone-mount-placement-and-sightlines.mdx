Phone mount buying advice covers the mechanism thoroughly and the placement barely at all,
which is backwards — placement is what determines whether the mount is safe to use.

## Two constraints, not one

**What it blocks.** Anything in the swept, forward-looking part of the windscreen is in
the way, and a phone is large enough to conceal a pedestrian at a junction.

**What's behind it.** Passenger airbags deploy through a seam in the upper dashboard. Side
curtain airbags deploy from the A-pillar trim down the window line. A mount adhered over
either path becomes a projectile at deployment.

The second one is the constraint people never consider, and it's the one with the worst
failure mode.

## The positions that work

**Low corner of the windscreen, driver's side.** Out of the forward view, close enough that
a glance needs eye movement rather than head movement.

**High on the centre dash**, near the top of the infotainment stack. Roughly where the
car's own screen sits, which is where the manufacturer already decided it was acceptable
to look.

Both keep the phone in a position you can take in briefly and return from quickly.

## The positions that don't

**Middle of the windscreen.** Directly in the sightline. Common because it's where the
suction cup sticks easily.

**Low centre console.** Requires looking properly down and away — the worst of the common
options, and the one that feels tidiest.

**Over any airbag panel.** See above.

**Blocking a demister vent.** Creates a permanently fogged patch exactly where you need
clear glass.

## The legal position varies

Several US states prohibit windscreen mounting outright; others allow it only within a
defined zone, typically a small area in a lower corner.

Most of those rules were written for suction-cup GPS units and apply to phone mounts the
same way. Worth checking your own state's wording rather than assuming — the variation is
wide and enforcement differs.

## Vent mounts and heat

Vent mounts put load on vane pivots designed to hold a plastic slat, not a phone. Heavy
phones cause sagging or a snapped vane over time.

They also sit the phone directly in the airflow, which means hot air in summer and cold in
winter. Combined with direct sun, that's how a phone hits its thermal limit and stops
charging or shuts down mid-navigation.

Positioning out of direct sun matters more than most people expect — the shaded band at
the top of a windscreen is a genuine advantage of that mounting location.

## Where the dash cam goes instead

Worth noting the two don't compete for the same space. A dash cam belongs high and central
behind the rear-view mirror, out of your sightline entirely, because you never look at it.
