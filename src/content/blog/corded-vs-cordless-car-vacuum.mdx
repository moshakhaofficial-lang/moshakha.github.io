Cordless vacuums are the ones people want, because nothing is more pleasant than no cable
across the driveway. They are also the ones that most often disappoint halfway through the
job.

## The real constraint: time

A proper interior vacuum means seats, rails, footwells, mats, the boot, the gaps beside
the console. **That's more time than most small cordless handhelds sustain at full
suction.**

Two things happen as the battery drains:

- **The motor loses power**, and suction falls off.
- **Filters clog**, which restricts airflow further. Fine dust is what kills airflow.

You notice it around the second footwell, which is exactly when you're reaching the
places that need it.

## What corded gives you

- **Consistent suction** for as long as you're working.
- **No charging schedule**: it's ready when you are.
- **Often a lower price** for equivalent power.

The downsides: **a cord to manage**, and in the 12V case **you need a socket that's live.**

## The socket trap

Many modern cars switch off the 12V socket with the ignition. If yours does, a corded 12V
vacuum runs only with the engine on, which is a poor idea in a closed garage. We explain
how to check in
[does the 12V socket stay on with the ignition off](/blog/does-12v-socket-stay-on-with-ignition-off/).
Check **before** you buy, not after.

## Who should choose what

- **Choose corded** for a full interior clean, dirty family cars, pets, anything that takes
  longer than a few minutes.
- **Choose cordless** for light upkeep: crumbs, a quick tidy, keeping one in the door
  pocket.
- **Use a household vacuum** if your car is parked near the house. It's often the most
  powerful of the three.

## One honest caveat

Budget car vacuums have modest suction next to a household machine, and embedded pet hair
needs a rubber brush first. Our [ThisWorx review](/reviews/thisworx-car-vacuum/) covers
the trade-offs, and [the interior detailing sequence](/blog/perfect-interior-detail/)

## Verdict

For most people, **corded wins on consistency.** Cordless wins on convenience for small
jobs, and you should pick based on which kind of cleaning you really do.
