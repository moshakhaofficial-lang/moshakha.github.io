Interior fog is simple physics: **moist air touches a surface colder than it is, and the
water comes out of the air.** The fix follows directly from that.

## The fast fix

1. **Turn the air conditioning on.** It dehumidifies the air, which is what you want.
2. **Switch off recirculation.** Recirculating keeps the same damp breath inside.
3. **Point the airflow at the glass** and raise the temperature. Warm, dry air holds
   moisture and lifts it off the glass.
4. **Crack a window** for a moment if it's really bad.

Air conditioning is the part people skip, because it feels backwards in winter. It's the
most effective lever, since it removes moisture rather than merely warming it.

## Where the moisture comes from

- **Your own breath.** A car full of people fogs fast.
- **Wet clothing and umbrellas.** They evaporate into a small sealed space.
- **Damp carpets and mats.** A quiet, constant moisture source that's easy to overlook.
  See [cleaning and drying floor mats](/how-to/how-to-clean-car-floor-mats/).
- **A blocked drain or leak.** If it's constant and there's a musty smell, something is
  letting water in.

## Why dirty glass fogs more

Interior glass gets a greasy film: off-gassing from plastics and vinyl, skin contact, and
smoke. **That film gives condensation something to grip**, so the fog forms in a sheet and
clears slowly.

## If it keeps happening

- **Check the cabin filter.** A clogged filter restricts airflow, so the demister is weaker.
  [Replacing it](/how-to/how-to-replace-cabin-air-filter/) is usually quick.
- **Check for damp carpet** under the mats and in the footwell.
- **Look for leaks** around door seals, the windscreen, and the sunroof drains.

## Bottom line

**Dry the air, clean the glass, find the moisture.** Most persistent fogging is a damp
carpet or a dirty windscreen rather than a fault in the car.
