Wiper blades get replaced on a schedule, which is odd for a part whose condition is
visible every time it rains. The useful question isn't how old it is — it's what pattern
it leaves.

## Read the symptom before buying anything

**Streaks in a repeating band**, appearing in the same place on every sweep — that's a
physical defect in the rubber edge. A nick, or a section that's taken a permanent set.
Replace the blade.

**Juddering and chattering** across the screen — usually **not** the blade. Most often
it's glass contamination, particularly road film or an old rain repellent breaking down
unevenly. Clean the screen properly first.

**A general smear** over the whole sweep with no repeating pattern — also usually the
glass. Frequently it's the interior film, which people don't think to check because the
wipers are outside.

Two of the three common complaints are a cleaning problem. Replacing blades fixes neither.

## Clean the glass properly first

This is the cheapest diagnostic available, and it resolves a meaningful share of wiper
complaints outright.

Road film builds a layer that wipers skip across rather than glide over. Old rain
repellent wears patchily and does the same. Neither comes off with a casual wipe — it
needs a proper decontamination of the glass surface.

## Age matters less than exposure

UV degrades wiper rubber faster than use does.

A blade on a garaged car in a mild climate can stay good well beyond a year. One on a car
parked outside in strong sun can be finished in six months having barely been used. The
six-to-twelve-month advice averages those two cases and describes neither.

Check the edge: rubber that's hardened, gone shiny, or split along its length is finished
regardless of the calendar.

## The winter damage people inflict themselves

Running wipers over a frozen screen tears the rubber edge where it's stuck to the glass,
and that damage is permanent.

It's the most common cause of a blade that streaks from one specific morning onward. Free
them by hand first — covered in
[de-icing a windshield safely](/how-to/how-to-deice-a-windshield-safely/).

Lifting the arms off the glass overnight in freezing weather avoids it entirely, though
it's worth checking the arms don't spring back hard enough to chip the screen.

## Beam blades versus frame blades

On modern curved screens, beam blades generally do better. A tensioned spring along the
length applies even pressure across the curve, where a traditional frame applies it at a
few contact points.

They also have no exposed hinges to pack with ice, which is a genuine winter advantage
rather than a marketing one.
