The marketing version says edgeless towels are premium and edged towels are cheap. The
reality is more useful than that, and it depends on what you're wiping.

## What the difference is

An **edged towel** has a border stitched or bound around the perimeter, often in satin or
polyester. An **edgeless towel** is cut cleanly, with the fibres finishing at the edge
rather than being wrapped in a seam.

That border is the whole story. It's **stiffer than the towel around it**, and it's the
part most likely to trap a piece of grit.

## Where it matters

**Paint and glass.** Anywhere a stray particle can leave a mark, you want a soft surface
touching the panel. This is why the glass and paint towels in a kit are the ones worth
buying edgeless, as our [glass cleaning guide](/blog/glass-cleaning-mastery/) recommends.

## Where it doesn't

**Wheels, jambs, engine bays, door shuts, general interior plastics.** A stitched edge
does no harm here, and these towels get contaminated anyway. Buy these cheap and treat them
as disposable.

This fits the broader approach in
[how many towels to detail a car](/blog/how-many-microfiber-towels-to-detail-a-car/):
**assign towels to jobs**, and spend the money where the surface is delicate.

## What to check before buying

- **How the edge is finished.** "Edgeless" should mean cut clean, not a thin stitched
  border sold under that name.
- **GSM in the right range for the job.** Lower GSM suits glass and buffing, higher suits
  drying. Weight isn't a quality score, as we explain in
  [microfibre care 101](/blog/microfibre-care-101/).
- **Whether the blend and pile feel soft** in the hand.

## Aftercare

Edgeless towels are still microfiber, so the same rules apply: **wash separately, skip the
fabric softener, dry on low.** Softener coats the fibres and ruins absorbency, which is
also the cause of most "my towels stopped working" complaints.

## Verdict

Edgeless is worth it for **paint and glass**, optional everywhere else. Don't pay a premium
for towels that will be used on wheels.
