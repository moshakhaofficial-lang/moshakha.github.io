Glass is where good detailing work most visibly falls apart. The paint looks perfect and
then the sun hits the windscreen and shows every pass you made.

The fix is almost never a different cleaner.

## It's the towel

The single most common cause of streaky glass is **using too much pile**.

Glass is perfectly smooth. A deep, plush towel can't press evenly against it — the fibres
bridge across the surface rather than making flat contact, so the last thin film of
moisture and cleaner gets moved around instead of lifted off.

That's why your 1200 GSM drying towel streaks windows even when it's spotlessly clean.
It isn't faulty. It's the wrong tool, and no glass cleaner compensates for it.

**What you want:** 300–400 GSM, low pile or waffle weave, edgeless. Cheap, and it fixes
the problem outright.

## The two-towel method

Even with the right towel, glass benefits from two:

1. **Towel one, slightly damp** — spray your cleaner onto the towel, not the glass, and
   clean the surface.
2. **Towel two, completely dry** — buff immediately, before the cleaner starts to
   evaporate.

The first towel removes the dirt. The second removes what the first one left behind.
This is the step that separates "mostly clean" from genuinely streak-free.

## Never in direct sun

If the glass is warm, the cleaner evaporates before you can buff it — and evaporation
leaves behind every dissolved solid that was in the product and the dirt. Those deposits
are the streaks.

Work in shade, on cool glass. If the windscreen is hot, wait.

## Technique details that matter

- **Spray the towel, not the glass.** Less overspray onto trim and paint, and it
  discourages using too much product.
- **Use less than feels right.** Excess cleaner leaves more residue than the towel can
  lift.
- **Work in one direction per side.** Horizontal on the outside, vertical on the inside.
  If a streak shows up later, you know instantly which side it's on.
- **Do the top edge last.** Lower the window an inch and clean the strip that hides in
  the seal — it's the bit that reappears as a dirty line the first time you open it.

## The inside of the windscreen

The hardest glass on the car, because it collects an oily film from interior plastics
off-gassing in the heat. Ordinary cleaner smears it around rather than cutting it.

Two passes: one to break down the film, one to finish. Keep a dedicated interior glass
towel that has **never** touched wax, sealant or trim dressing — a contaminated towel
will deposit product onto the screen and make it worse than when you started.

## Tint and ammonia

Ammonia-based household glass cleaner damages window tint over time and degrades rubber
seals and some interior plastics. If the car has aftermarket tint, ammonia will
eventually cause it to bubble and discolour.

Use an ammonia-free automotive formula. It's not a meaningful price difference.
