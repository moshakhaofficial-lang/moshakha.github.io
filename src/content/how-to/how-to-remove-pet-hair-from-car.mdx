Pet hair is the one interior cleaning job where doing the obvious thing first makes it
harder. Reaching for the vacuum is the instinct, and it's why so many people conclude
their car vacuum is useless.

## Why suction alone doesn't work

Loose debris sits on top of carpet. Pet hair doesn't — it works its way between the fibers
and wraps around them. Once that's happened, it's mechanically tangled, not merely
resting there.

**A vacuum can only lift what's free to move.** That's true of a shop vac as much as a
handheld, which is why more suction rarely solves this. The hair has to be freed first.

## Rubber does the actual work

A rubber brush, a squeegee edge, or a plain rubber glove generates the friction and static
that rolls woven hair back up out of the pile. Plastic bristles skim across the top of it.

Pull in **one consistent direction**. Sweeping back and forth lifts hair on the forward
stroke and pushes it straight back in on the return. Pull toward one edge repeatedly and
the hair gathers into a clump you can pick up.

## Then collect by hand, then vacuum

Bin the clumps before the vacuum comes out. A handheld car vacuum will clog on a
fist-sized wad of hair, and clearing the filter costs more time than collecting by hand.

Once the bulk is gone, the vacuum is doing what it's actually good at — picking up loose
hair and grit — rather than fighting the carpet.

## Where a pumice block earns its place

For hair that's deeply woven into carpet, a dedicated pet hair pumice block grabs what
rubber can't. It's mildly abrasive by design, so keep it to carpet and durable woven
fabric with light pressure — never leather, vinyl, suede or Alcantara.

## The only real prevention

A washable seat cover or fitted cargo liner. It gives the hair somewhere to collect that
you can lift out and shake off, instead of somewhere it can weave in.
