A steering wheel is the surface you touch more than any other in the car, and it's
usually the last thing anyone cleans.

## The shine isn't dirt

That glossy patch at ten and two is **skin oil, sunscreen and hand cream**, transferred
over thousands of contacts and compressed into a smooth layer.

It isn't dirt in the ordinary sense, which is why wiping with a dry cloth achieves
nothing. Summer makes it dramatically worse — sunscreen and hand lotion transfer far more
readily than skin oil alone, which is why wheels look worst at the end of a hot season.

## It's a grip problem first

Worth being clear about, because it's usually framed as cosmetic.

**The glossy layer is genuinely more slippery than the original surface texture.** That
matters with wet hands or in a sudden manoeuvre. On a leather wheel it also traps moisture
and grit against the material, which is what eventually wears the coating through at the
contact points.

## Work out what the rim is

Most wheels are coated leather or polyurethane — both behave like a sealed surface and
want a pH-neutral cleaner. Genuine uncoated leather is rare.

The same water-drop test that settles this on seats works here: beading means coated,
absorbing means bare leather. The full reasoning is in
[cleaning leather car seats](/how-to/leather-seat-care/).

## Spray the towel, not the wheel

Overspray from a steering wheel lands on the instrument cluster lens, the stalks, and the
airbag cover — all surfaces you'd rather not coat in cleaner.

Damp the towel, work a section at a time, and use a soft brush on the stitching and the
seam where the rim halves meet — that's where residue concentrates and where a towel
simply bridges across.

## Never put gloss on the rim

This is the one interior surface where the usual aesthetic argument doesn't apply.

A glossy dressing on a steering wheel makes it slippery, and it reflects sunlight up at
you from a surface directly in your field of view. If the rim needs protection at all, use
a **matte** protectant, applied thinly and buffed off properly.

The same finish argument applies across the dashboard, for a related reason — that's in
[dashboard dressing and windshield glare](/blog/dashboard-dressing-glare/).

## Do it monthly

Build-up is far easier to remove early than after months of being polished in by your own
hands. Monthly on a daily driver keeps it from ever reaching the glossy stage.
