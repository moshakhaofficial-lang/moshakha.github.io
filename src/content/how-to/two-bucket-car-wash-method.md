If you do only one thing differently, do this. The two-bucket method costs about the
price of one bucket and prevents more paint damage than any product you can buy.

## What the second bucket is for

A single bucket has an obvious problem that's easy to miss while you're doing it.

You dunk a clean mitt, wash a panel, and the mitt comes back carrying road grime. You
dunk it again — into your soap. Now the soap has grit in it. Four panels later you're
washing your car with a bucket of dilute sandpaper, and every subsequent panel gets
worse.

**The second bucket gives the dirt somewhere else to go.** Rinse the mitt in plain water
first, *then* reload it with soap. Your soap bucket stays clean from start to finish.

That's the entire method. Everything else is refinement.

## Grit guards

A grit guard is a plastic grate that sits in the bottom of the bucket. Dirt settles
below it, and the grate stops your mitt reaching that dirt when you dunk.

Without one, the rinse bucket does half a job: dirt goes in, then gets stirred back into
suspension the next time you plunge a mitt through it. They cost very little and one in
each bucket is the sensible setup.

## The order matters as much as the buckets

**Wheels first, always, with separate tools.** Brake dust is metallic and scratches
paint on contact. There is no version of this where the same mitt does wheels and
panels.

**Then top to bottom.** Roof, glass, upper panels, then doors, then the lower third and
bumpers. Gravity means the bottom of your car holds most of the grit, and working
downward keeps that grit away from panels you've already cleaned.

**Straight lines, never circles.** Any marring that does happen will be far less visible
as parallel lines than as overlapping arcs. Circular motion is what turns light scratches
into the halo pattern people call swirl marks.

## The mistakes that undo it

- **Washing in direct sun.** Shampoo dries on the panel, you scrub to remove it, and the
  scrubbing does the damage.
- **A mitt that isn't loaded enough.** Lubrication protects the paint. A damp mitt drags.
- **Pressing harder on stubborn dirt.** Rinse the panel again instead. Pressure is what
  turns a trapped particle into a scratch.
- **Continuing with a dropped mitt.** It has picked up sharp grit from the ground.
  Retire it until it's been laundered.
- **Letting the car air-dry.** Water spots are mineral deposits, and removing them is a
  harder job than avoiding them.

Any decent mitt works — the method matters more than the media. If you're still using a

the two-bucket method cannot fully compensate for a tool that traps grit against your
