Air vents are the detail that separates an interior that looks clean from one that
actually is — and the place where the obvious method makes things worse.

## Compressed air is the wrong instinct

Reaching for an air compressor or a can of compressed air feels right. It isn't.

Air blows dust **off** the slats and **into** the ducting behind them. From there it
distributes through the system and settles back out of every vent in the car over the
following days. You've moved the problem somewhere you can't reach and delayed it.

**Suction removes dust from the system.** That's the difference, and it's why the order
below starts with a vacuum rather than a blast.

## Vents before the dashboard

Whatever comes out of a vent lands on the console and dash below it.

Clean the dash first and you'll be cleaning it twice. This is the most common sequencing
mistake in an interior detail, and it's the same top-down logic that governs the rest of
the job — covered in [the interior detailing guide](/blog/perfect-interior-detail/).

## Brush with the vacuum held underneath

The technique that actually works uses both hands: detail brush between the vanes, crevice
tool held just below the vent catching what comes free.

It feels awkward for about ten seconds and then becomes obvious. Everything you dislodge
gets captured in the moment rather than settling somewhere you'll have to clean again.

Boar bristle matters here specifically because vent vanes are **thin plastic on a small
pivot**. They snap rather than bend. A soft brush lets the bristle tips work between the
slats without you levering against them.

## Never spray into a vent

Interior detailer goes onto the swab, not into the opening.

Liquid that gets past the slats lands in ducting with no airflow to dry it. That's how you
create a damp smell rather than remove one — and it's exactly the mistake that leads
people to think they need an air purifier.

## When the smell isn't the vents

If the smell arrives **with the fan**, particularly with the air conditioning on, the
vents aren't the source. It's mould on the evaporator behind the dash, or a cabin filter
long overdue.

Cleaning the slats won't touch either. Start with
[replacing the cabin air filter](/how-to/how-to-replace-cabin-air-filter/) — it's ten
minutes and it's almost certainly due.

## Finish with the fan on high

Doors open, fan at maximum for a minute. Anything you loosened deeper in comes out and
leaves the car, rather than settling back onto a dashboard you've just finished.
