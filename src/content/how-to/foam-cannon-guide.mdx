A foam cannon gets sold as a shortcut, and used that way, it disappoints. Used correctly,
it's a genuine risk-reduction step ahead of a normal contact wash — not a replacement
for one.

## What pre-foaming actually does

Contact washing — a mitt dragged across paint — is where swirl marks come from, because
grit sitting on the surface gets dragged along with the mitt under friction. Pre-foaming
coats the car in thick, clinging suds that dwell on the surface for a minute or two,
softening and starting to lift that grit before any mitt ever touches the paint.

**Rinse the foam off, and a meaningful share of that loosened grit goes with it.** What's
left for the contact wash to deal with is less than what would have been there without
the pre-foam step.

## What it doesn't do

**It doesn't replace contact washing.** Bonded contamination, grime worked into panel
gaps, and anything that's had time to etch into the surface won't lift with foam alone.
A full [two-bucket wash](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method/) still follows every time.

**It doesn't work without dwell time.** Spraying foam on and rinsing immediately skips
the part where the foam actually does its job. Give it a minute or two before rinsing.

**It isn't a substitute for the right soap.** Not every car shampoo aerates well through
a cannon — check that whatever you're using is rated for foam cannon use, since the
dilution ratio typically differs from bucket-wash use of the same product.

## Getting the dwell time right

Watch the foam rather than setting a fixed timer. In cool, shaded conditions a couple of
minutes is fine. In direct sun or hot weather, foam can start drying on the surface
faster than expected — and dried foam residue is its own cleanup problem, sometimes
worse than the dirt it was meant to loosen. Rinse before it looks like it's starting to
dry, not after.

## The full sequence

Foam, dwell, rinse thoroughly, then wash normally with clean water, a grit guard, and the
[two-bucket method](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method/). Pre-foaming is a step that
makes the wash after it safer — it isn't a wash on its own.
