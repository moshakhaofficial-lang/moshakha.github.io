A streaking microfibre towel is one of the most common complaints in detailing, and it's
almost never a faulty towel. It's one of three things, and they're easy to tell apart.

## Diagnose it first

**Does water bead on the towel instead of soaking in?**
→ Contamination. The fibres are coated. Go to the strip wash.

**Is it only glass that streaks, while paint is fine?**
→ Pile depth. You're using too plush a towel on a smooth surface.

**Does it streak on everything, and the towel has been used for wax or dressings?**
→ Product contamination. That towel is carrying product in its pile.

## Cause 1: fabric softener

The most common cause, and the most preventable.

Softener works by depositing a waxy film on fibres. On a bath towel that feels pleasant.
On microfibre it fills the microscopic gaps that draw water in — so the towel stops
absorbing and starts pushing water around. Worse, that waxy film transfers onto whatever
you're wiping, which is the streak you're seeing.

Dryer sheets do exactly the same thing.

**Fix:** strip wash. Towels alone, no detergent, a cup of white vinegar in the wash, then
a second full rinse. Repeat once if the improvement is partial. Then never use softener
on microfibre again — full routine in our
[microfibre care guide](/blog/microfibre-care-101/).

## Cause 2: too much pile for the surface

Glass is perfectly smooth. A deep, plush pile can't make even contact with it — the
fibres bridge across the surface rather than pressing flat against it, so the last thin
film of moisture gets moved around rather than lifted.

This is why a 1200 GSM drying towel streaks windows no matter how clean it is. It isn't
faulty; it's the wrong tool.

**Fix:** a 300-400 GSM low-pile or waffle weave towel for glass. This single purchase
resolves more streaking complaints than any glass cleaner, and it costs a few dollars.
See [what GSM actually means](/guides/microfiber-gsm-explained/) for why the ranges work
this way.

## Cause 3: product contamination

A towel used to buff off wax, sealant or trim dressing holds that product deep in its
pile. Washing helps but rarely removes it completely — and if you wash that towel
alongside your glass towels, you've now contaminated those too.

**Fix:** launder microfibre in separate categories — glass, paint, wax, wheels — and
never mix them. Colour-coding by job is the simplest way to keep this honest.

## The technique that fixes the rest

Even with a clean, correct towel, glass benefits from **two towels**:

1. One slightly damp low-pile towel to clean.
2. A second, completely dry low-pile towel to buff immediately after.

The first removes the dirt; the second removes what the first left behind. Nearly all
residual streaking disappears here.

Two other things worth checking:

- **Use less product.** Spray onto the towel, not the glass, and use less than feels
  right. Excess cleaner is itself a streak source.
- **Never do glass in direct sun.** The cleaner evaporates before you can buff it,
  leaving its dissolved solids behind as streaks.

## If nothing works

If a strip wash makes no difference and the towel feels stiff or slightly crunchy rather
than plush, the fibres have been melted by heat at some point — a hot wash or a hot
dryer. Fused fibres can't be recovered. Retire it to door shuts and wheel arches and
start again, laundering the replacement properly from day one.
