This is the cheapest meaningful improvement you can make to a car's interior, it takes
about ten minutes, and on most vehicles it needs no tools at all. It's also the
maintenance item people are least likely to have ever done.

## Where it is

Behind the glovebox, on the large majority of cars. A minority put it under the dash on
the passenger side, or up in the cowl area beneath the windscreen where the wiper arms
sit.

**Check the manual before dismantling anything.** Two minutes of reading saves taking
apart a panel that was never hiding a filter.

## Getting the glovebox out of the way

This is the part that looks intimidating and isn't. Empty it completely, then find the
**damper arm** — a small rod or fabric strap on one side that stops the box dropping too
far. Unclip it.

Then squeeze the side walls of the glovebox inward with both hands. The moulded stops
clear the frame and the whole box swings down, exposing a rectangular plastic cover.

Nothing here is fragile in the way it feels. The plastic is designed to flex for exactly
this job.

## Photograph the old one before it comes out

There's an **airflow direction arrow** printed on the filter's frame. The new one has to
point the same way.

A photo takes two seconds and removes any doubt later. Fitted backwards, the filter still
works — but with reduced airflow and a shorter life, because the pleat structure loads
from one face by design.

## Expect it to be worse than you thought

Pull it out slowly and tip it downward as it clears the housing, so the debris it's
carrying falls into the footwell rather than back into the ductwork.

Most first-time replacements come out grey or brown, packed with leaf fragments and seed
cases. That's not neglect on your part so much as an item nobody mentions at service
time.

While the housing is open, run a crevice tool along the bottom of it. Debris collects
there and loads the new filter prematurely.

A soft brush is useful for the housing edges and the vent slots nearby, where a vacuum

## If the smell doesn't go away

A new filter fixes a filter-shaped problem. If the smell specifically appears **when the
air conditioning starts**, the likely source is mould on the evaporator behind the
dashboard — which is downstream of the filter and unaffected by replacing it.
