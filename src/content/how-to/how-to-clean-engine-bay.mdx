Engine bay cleaning has a reputation for being risky, and the reputation comes almost
entirely from people using pressure washers on it.

## The bay already handles water

A modern engine bay lives through rain, road spray and deep puddles. Components down
there are sealed for exactly that. Water itself is not the threat.

**Concentrated pressure is.** A pressure washer can force water past a seal that shrugs
off rain all winter, push it into a connector, dislodge clips and lift insulation pads.

So the method here is: normal hose pressure, and let brushes do the work that a jet would
otherwise be doing.

## Cover these, skip the rest

There's a short list worth protecting, and a long list that doesn't need it.

**Cover:** alternator, exposed electrical connectors, air intake opening and filter
housing, fuse box lids, battery terminals.

**Don't bother covering:** painted metal, plastic engine covers, inner wings, slam panel,
hoses, coolant and washer reservoirs, the underside of the bonnet.

Plastic bags or foil, tucked or taped. It takes five minutes and removes most of the
remaining risk.

## Warm engine, not hot

Slightly warm helps the cleaner work and speeds the drying afterwards.

**Hot is a genuine hazard.** Cold water hitting a hot exhaust manifold or turbo housing
risks thermal shock, and you can't safely reach around hot components in the first place.
About twenty minutes after a short drive is right — warm to the touch, not uncomfortable.

## Brushes do the work

This is where an engine bay differs from paint. There's no marring risk on a cast
aluminium block or a plastic cover, so you can agitate properly.

A long-handled brush reaches the back of the bay and down the sides of the block, which
is where the grime you can see from above actually lives.

## Run the engine afterwards

Towel what you can reach, pull your coverings off, then **run the engine for ten minutes
with the bonnet up.**

The heat drives moisture out of casting recesses and around connectors where no towel
reaches. Skipping this is why people occasionally get a rough idle or a warning light
after a wash — usually temporary damp rather than damage, and entirely avoidable.

## On dressing the plastics

Optional, and if you do it, matte or satin rather than gloss. A glossy engine bay reads
as wet rather than clean, holds dust, and can migrate when the bay heats up.
