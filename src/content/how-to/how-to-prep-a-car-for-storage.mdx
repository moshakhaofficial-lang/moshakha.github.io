Storage advice usually starts with paint protection. In practice, the thing that's
actually flat, seized or smelling when you come back is rarely the paint.

## The battery is what fails

Modern cars draw current constantly even switched off — alarm, keyless entry, various
control modules staying in a low-power state. Over months, that drains a battery
completely, and a battery left deeply discharged for a long period often doesn't recover.

**A maintainer is the right answer** if there's mains power where the car sits. It holds
the charge without overcharging, and the car keeps its settings.

**Disconnecting the negative terminal** is the fallback with no power available. It stops
the parasitic drain, though the battery still self-discharges slowly on its own.

## Don't start it "to keep it healthy"

This is the most common well-intentioned mistake.

A brief start never gets the engine and exhaust hot enough to drive off the water that
combustion produces. That moisture condenses in the oil and the exhaust system and sits
there. You've added contamination without achieving circulation.

**Either drive it properly for twenty minutes or more, or leave it alone.** A five-minute
idle in the driveway is the worst of both.

## Wash and protect before, not after

Contamination left on paint has the whole storage period to work. Bird droppings and sap
etch permanently over months; brake dust corrodes into wheel finishes.

A wash, a decontamination pass and a coat of sealant before it sits is far more valuable
than the same work after.

## Pressures a little high

Tyres lose pressure slowly, and a tyre sitting under-inflated on one spot for months can
take a flat spot. Most settle after some miles; a long enough period can leave a permanent
vibration.

A few PSI above the placard figure gives margin for the slow loss. Sizing and technique
in [best tire inflators](/guides/best-tire-inflators/).

## Handbrake off, wheels chocked

Rear pads seize to discs when held in contact for months in damp conditions. Releasing the
handbrake and chocking the wheels avoids it.

On cars with an electronic parking brake this is more awkward and the manual may
specifically say to leave it engaged — follow that if so.

## Breathable cover, or none

A breathable cover keeps dust off and lets moisture escape.

**A plastic tarpaulin traps condensation against the paint** and holds it there all
winter. That does more damage than no cover at all. Indoors and uncovered beats outdoors
under plastic, every time.
