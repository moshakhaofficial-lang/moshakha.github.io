More windscreens are damaged by their owners in winter than by stones, and almost all of
it comes from one shortcut.

## Hot water is the thing not to do

Pouring hot water on very cold glass creates a steep temperature gradient across it. The
resulting stress propagates from any existing chip or edge flaw into a crack that runs
across the screen.

Modern laminated glass is tough. It is not immune to thermal shock, and the failure
happens in an instant with no warning.

Lukewarm water is less risky and still unnecessary. The demister and a plastic scraper do
the job without gambling on the screen.

## Demister on first, then scrape

Engine running, fan at full, directed at the screen, **fresh air rather than recirculate**,
and air conditioning on if the car has it.

The AC part surprises people. The compressor dehumidifies the air, and dry air clears
interior fog far faster than warm humid air. Recirculate does the opposite — it keeps
recycling the moisture you're breathing out.

That works from the inside while you scrape the outside.

## Plastic scraper, straight strokes

A proper scraper is shaped to clear ice without marking glass. Metal blades scratch it,
credit cards snap, and kitchen implements do both.

Overlapping straight passes work better than short scrubbing motions, and put less stress
through any chip you already have.

## Leave the wipers until last

Wipers frozen to the screen **tear their rubber edges** when the motor pulls against the
ice. That damage is permanent, and it's the cause of streaking that people then blame on
the blade being old.

Free them by hand or with de-icer fluid before switching them on. Signs a blade is
genuinely finished rather than just frozen are in
[when to replace windshield wipers](/blog/when-to-replace-windshield-wipers/).

## Clear all of it, not a porthole

A cleared patch in front of the driver is unsafe and, in many jurisdictions, an offence.

Full windscreen, both front side windows, mirrors, rear screen — and brush the snow off
the roof, or it slides down over the glass at your first hard stop.

## The interior film matters more in winter

That greasy haze on the inside of the glass is partly off-gassed residue from interior
plastics, and it gives moisture something to cling to. It's why some cars fog and frost on
the inside far more readily than others.
