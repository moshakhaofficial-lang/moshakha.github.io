Winter salt stains are the one interior cleaning problem that needs almost no chemistry
and almost always gets the wrong technique.

## Salt dissolves — so scrubbing isn't the answer

Road salt is water-soluble. That's the whole mechanism.

The failure people run into is dissolving it without ever **removing** it. Wetting a salt
stain spreads dissolved salt through the damp area; if that moisture then dries in place,
the salt recrystallises and the white ring comes back, often larger than it started.

**Extraction is the step that actually removes salt from the carpet.** Everything else is
preparation for it.

## Vacuum before any moisture

Dry salt crystals are abrasive, and feet grind them into the pile.

Wetting them before vacuuming turns loose crystals into a paste that sinks deeper.
Vacuum first — including along the sill and under the seat rails, where it collects out
of sight.

## Warm water on the brush, not the carpet

Damp the brush rather than soaking the footwell. That puts moisture exactly where the
agitation is happening without driving salt down into the foam backing.

A little white vinegar helps with stubborn residue. A strong carpet cleaner doesn't —
it just adds surfactant that also needs extracting afterwards.

## Press to extract, don't wipe

Fold a dry microfiber, press it firmly into the agitated area, and hold. Refold to a clean
face and repeat.

Wiping smears dissolved salt around. Pressing draws it up into the towel. Same technique
as fabric seats, and for the same reason —
[cleaning fabric car seats](/how-to/how-to-clean-car-seats-fabric/) covers the
over-wetting trap in more detail.

## Two light passes beat one soak

Over-wetting pushes salt into the underlay where nothing you do at the surface will reach
it, and leaves a footwell that takes days to dry.

Dry thoroughly afterwards — doors open or a fan into the footwell until it's dry to the
touch throughout.

## The real fix is next winter

Cleaning carpet repeatedly is treating the symptom. **All-weather mats with a raised lip**
contain slush instead of letting it soak through, and shaking them out regularly beats any
amount of extraction work.

damp carpet is how rust starts from the inside. Mat options are in
