Exhaust tips are the one exterior detail where the wrong product causes damage that
cannot be undone — and the two common finishes want opposite treatment.

## Cold, first and always

This isn't a paint-safety caution. Exhaust tips hold heat long after the engine is off,
and a soot layer hides how hot the metal underneath still is.

If you've been driving, leave it overnight.

## Identify the finish before anything abrasive

**Polished stainless or chrome** — bright, reflective, takes metal polish and fine steel
wool. These can be brought back to a mirror finish with effort.

**Black, matte or ceramic-coated** — increasingly common as a factory fitment. Polish
strips the coating, exposing the metal beneath, which then discolours unevenly with heat.
There's no way back from that short of re-coating.

**If you can't tell, assume coated** and stay non-abrasive. The cost of being wrong in
that direction is a tip that stays matte. The cost of being wrong the other way is a
ruined finish.

## Degrease first — it's often the whole job

Much of what reads as permanent staining is oily soot sitting on the surface.

A degreaser or alkaline wheel cleaner plus a brush removes it, and on plenty of tips that
alone gets you to a result you're happy with. Doing this before reaching for abrasives
tells you how much work actually remains.

Avoid acidic wheel cleaners here, for the same reason they're wrong on polished wheels —
they attack stainless and any coating present. The pH argument is in
[wheel cleaner pH](/blog/wheel-cleaner-ph-explained/).

## For stainless, work in stages

Metal polish on a cloth first. If baked carbon resists, move to fine steel wool using the
polish as lubricant, working with the direction of any existing grain.

Expect the cloth to come away black repeatedly before it starts lightening. That's normal
— you're removing layers of carbon, not polishing metal, until quite late in the process.

## These tools never touch paint again

Exhaust soot is oily and carbon-loaded. Steel wool sheds fine particles that will scratch
anything they contact afterwards.

Keep a dedicated brush for this job, store it away from the paint and wheel tools, and
throw the towels away rather than laundering them with everything else. Same separation
discipline as wheels, covered in [wheel and rim care](/blog/wheel-rim-care/).

## On blue and gold tinting

That's heat discolouration in the stainless itself rather than a deposit — the surface
oxide layer changing under sustained high temperature.

Light tinting sometimes reduces with polish. Heavy tinting is effectively permanent, and
it's normal rather than a fault.
