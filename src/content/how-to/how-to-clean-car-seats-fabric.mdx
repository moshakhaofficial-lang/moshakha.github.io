There are two ways to clean a fabric seat. One leaves it clean and dry in an hour. The
other leaves it clean, damp for a week, and smelling musty by the following weekend.

The difference isn't the product. It's how much water gets into the foam.

## The foam is the problem

Fabric seating sits on open-cell foam, which absorbs water readily and releases it slowly.
Inside a closed car there's no airflow underneath and very little above. A seat that gets
genuinely soaked can stay damp internally for days.

Warm, dark, enclosed, and damp is precisely the condition that produces a musty interior.
So the goal isn't only to clean the fabric — it's to clean it while introducing as little
liquid as possible.

## Spray the brush, not the seat

This one change does most of the work.

Spraying the seat directly saturates the fabric and pushes cleaner straight through into
the foam. Spraying the brush puts the cleaner exactly where the agitation happens — at the
fiber surface, where the dirt actually is — and almost nothing reaches the foam.

## Vacuum first, always

Cleaner applied over loose grit makes mud and drives it deeper into the weave. Vacuum
everything first, including the seam where the backrest meets the base, and under the
rails where debris collects.

## Press to extract — don't wipe

After agitating a small section, press a folded dry microfiber firmly into it and hold.
Wiping smears suspended dirt around; pressing draws it up into the towel. Refold to a
clean face each time and repeat until the towel comes away only lightly marked.

Work in sections about the size of a dinner plate, so nothing dries back into the fabric
before you extract it.

## Get the dilution right, and err weak

Use the product's **fabric** dilution, not its general-purpose one. Excess surfactant that
doesn't get extracted stays behind, attracts dirt faster afterwards, and can leave the
fabric feeling stiff. Weaker cleaner with more agitation beats stronger cleaner with less.

## Then ventilate properly

Doors open, or a fan pointed into the cabin, until the fabric is dry to the touch
throughout. Closing the car up on a seat that's still damp inside undoes an otherwise
good job.
