The install itself isn't the hard part — mounting a camera to glass and running a cable
is straightforward either way. The decision that actually matters is **hardwire or 12V
socket**, because it determines whether your dash cam can protect you while parked.

## The decision that comes before the install

A **12V socket plug** powers the camera only when the car is running, or when the
ignition is in accessory/on position. Five-minute install, zero risk of draining the
battery, and zero parking-mode recording. If someone hits your parked car, it isn't
recording.

**Hardwiring** to the fuse box is what enables parking mode — recording (or motion-
triggered recording) while the car is off. It takes longer, involves trim panels, and
if wired to the wrong kind of fuse, can drain your battery.

If you don't care about parking-mode footage, the 12V socket is the correct choice and
you can skip straight to mounting. If you want it, hardwire — there's no version of a
12V plug that gives you parking mode.

## The always-on-fuse mistake

This is the mistake that actually costs people a flat battery: tapping an **always-on**
fuse with a plain fuse-tap kit and no voltage-sensing hardwire kit. The camera draws a
small but constant current, and over a parked weekend that adds up to a battery too flat
to start the car.

The fix is one of two things: tap a **switched** fuse (powered only with the ignition
on — parking mode won't work, but the battery is safe), or use a proper hardwire kit
with a built-in low-voltage cutoff, which powers parking mode until the battery reaches
a safe threshold and then cuts off automatically.

## Grounding matters more than it looks like it should

A dash cam hardwire kit needs a ground connection, and the temptation is to screw it
into whatever metal is closest. Use an existing factory ground point instead — most cars
have several accessible under the dash or behind a kick panel. A poor ground causes
intermittent recording dropouts that look like a camera fault and are actually a wiring
fault, which makes them much harder to diagnose after the install is buttoned up.

## Before you close the trim back up

Test the install with the trim **off** first, confirm the camera powers on and records,
then reassemble. Testing only after full reassembly means any wiring mistake costs you a
second trim removal to fix it.

Once it's back together, park, lock the car, and confirm parking mode actually triggers
as expected — in your driveway, not after the incident it was supposed to catch.
