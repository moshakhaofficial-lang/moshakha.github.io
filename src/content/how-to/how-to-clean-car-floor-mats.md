Floor mats are the dirtiest part of a car interior and the part most often cleaned
badly — usually by hosing them down and putting them back before they're dry.

## Take them out. Every time.

Cleaning mats in the car pushes water and dirt into the carpet and underlay beneath
them. That underlay does not dry, and trapped moisture there is the single most common
source of a musty car smell that no amount of air freshener touches.

Thirty seconds to pull them out saves a problem that is genuinely difficult to reverse.

## Rubber and carpet want opposite things

This is the part that matters, and most guides treat mats as one category.

| | **Rubber / TPE liners** | **Carpet mats** |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaner | Rubber-specific | Carpet cleaner or diluted APC |
| Method | Scrub hard with stiff brush | Agitate lightly, then extract |
| Water | Rinse thoroughly | Use as little as possible |
| Drying | 1–2 hours in airflow | Up to a full day |
| Finish | **Matte only** | N/A |
| Avoid | Silicone gloss dressings | Soaking — the backing holds it |

Treat a rubber liner like carpet and it goes chalky. Treat a carpet mat like rubber and
you soak a backing that takes a day to dry.

## The gloss warning

**Never put tyre shine or a silicone dressing on a floor mat.**

It looks good for a day. Then your foot slips off the brake pedal on a wet morning
because the mat under it is slick. This is the one place in a car where shine is
actively dangerous, and it is worth being blunt about because "restore that new look"
marketing does not carry the warning.

If you want protection, use a matte rubber protector formulated for mats. If you're not
sure what a product will leave behind, use nothing.

## Chalky grey liners

People assume this is age. Usually it is **cleaner left in the tread channels**, dried
to a white residue.

Rinse rubber liners standing on edge, from the top down, so water carries residue and
grit off rather than pooling in the tread. Rinse for longer than feels necessary. A
surprising amount of what looks like permanent degradation rinses away.

UV is the other cause, and that one is real — a rubber protector slows it.

## Drying is the step that matters

A mat put back damp traps moisture against the carpet underneath. That is how mildew
starts, and mildew in underlay is very hard to remove.

Stand them on edge or hang them, in moving air, and give carpet mats a full day if you
can. A carpet mat that feels dry on the surface is often still wet against its rubber
backing.

## While the mats are out

Vacuum the footwell they were covering — that's where the grit that works through
collects. And **check for damp**.

A wet footwell under dry mats is not a spill. It is almost always a leaking door or
window seal, or a blocked sunroof or scuttle drain. Finding that early is worth far more
than clean mats.
