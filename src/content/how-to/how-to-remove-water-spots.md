Two different problems get called "water spots," and they need different fixes. Fresh
mineral deposits sitting on the surface come off with the right product. Etched water
spots are already physical damage to the clear coat, and no amount of chemical remover
fixes damage that's already happened.

## Tell them apart before you start

Run a fingernail lightly across a dry spot. **Smooth, with only a visible mark** —
that's a fresh deposit sitting on top of the clear coat. **A slight roughness or a ring
you can feel** — the mineral content has already etched into the clear coat itself.
This distinction determines everything that follows; treating an etched spot like a
fresh one just wastes product and time.

## Fresh deposits: dissolve, don't wipe

A dedicated water spot remover is formulated to dissolve mineral content specifically —
plain water, glass cleaner, or a general all-purpose cleaner often just relocates the
deposit rather than removing it. Apply on a cool panel out of direct sun, follow the
dwell time, and buff with a **separate, clean** microfiber towel — reusing the applicator
towel for buffing can redeposit the mineral content you just dissolved.

If spots persist after that, a clay bar pass afterward — not instead of the chemical
step — can lift deposits sitting slightly proud of the surface that the remover alone
didn't fully clear.

## Etched spots: only polish fixes this

If the fingernail test showed texture, you're looking at actual clear coat damage, and
the fix is leveling the surrounding clear coat with a fine cutting polish until the
etched ring is gone. This removes a very thin layer of clear coat in the process, which
is normal for light correction work but not something to repeat indiscriminately.
Comfortable with polishing? Do it by hand or machine. Not comfortable? This is a
reasonable point to hand off to a professional rather than risk uneven correction.

## The prevention that actually works

Water spots form because mineral-laden water was left to evaporate on the surface
instead of being wiped off while still wet — heat accelerates this, which is why spots
form fastest on a hot panel in direct sun. **Prompt, thorough drying after every wash or
rain exposure** is the real prevention, more reliable than any spot-resistant product
applied after the fact. See [best car drying towels](/guides/best-car-drying-towels/)
for tools built specifically to dry quickly and completely.

A hydrophobic wax or ceramic coating helps water bead and run off faster, reducing how
long it sits — but it doesn't eliminate the risk on its own if water is still left to
stand and evaporate.
