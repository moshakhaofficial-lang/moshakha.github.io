Winter car care advice usually leads with wax. That is the wrong end of the problem.

## What actually damages a car in winter

Road salt corrodes metal by staying in contact with it, in the presence of moisture, for
extended periods. The damage happens in the underbody, the wheel arches, and the seams —
places where nobody applies wax, and where the salt sits undisturbed for weeks.

**Rinsing removes the corrosive agent. Wax only makes it release a little more easily.**
That is the entire reason rinsing frequency outranks every product decision here.

## The order worth spending effort in

**First: rinse regularly.** Weekly while roads are being salted. Even a plain rinse
without a contact wash achieves most of the benefit, because the goal is removing salt,
not producing a clean-looking car.

**Second: cover the arches and underbody.** A top-down rinse that ignores them leaves
the salt exactly where it does the most damage. If your hose or pressure washer can reach
underneath, that is the highest-value minute of the whole wash.

**Third: protection.** Applied in late autumn, before the season. Wax and sealant need a
clean, dry, reasonably warm panel to bond — applying either in January gives poor
adhesion and a short service life.

## Why a wash-and-wax makes sense mid-season

Mid-winter is not the time for a full decontamination and hand wax. A shampoo with wax in
it adds a small amount of protection during the wash you were doing anyway, which is a
realistic way to maintain some protection through a season where ideal conditions never
arrive. It is not a substitute for a proper autumn application — it is maintenance
between them.

## Winter is when swirl marks are easiest to inflict

Winter grime is gritty and carries salt crystals. Contact washing without a thorough
pre-rinse drags all of that across the paint. Pre-rinse properly, and if you have a
pressure washer, pre-foam before touching anything — the reasoning is in
[avoiding swirl marks](/how-to/how-to-avoid-swirl-marks/).

## The part people forget

Dry the door and boot seals, not just the panels. Water left in a seal refreezes
overnight, and a frozen seal either holds the door shut or tears when forced. Flat panels
dry on their own; seals do not. More on the specifics in
[drying a car in freezing weather](/blog/drying-towel-for-winter/).
