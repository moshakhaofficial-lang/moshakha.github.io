Tree sap gets treated as a single problem with a single solution. It's really three
problems, and the only thing that separates them is how long the sap has been sitting
there.

## Work out which one you have

**Fresh and still soft** — a fingernail pressed at the edge gives slightly. Warm soapy
water will lift it.

**Hardened but sitting on top** — glassy and firm, but the paint underneath is still
smooth. Needs a solvent.

**Etched** — after removal, you can feel a ring or see a dull patch. The sap has damaged
the clear coat. Only polishing fixes this.

Checking takes seconds and determines everything that follows.

## Always try water first

A microfiber soaked in warm soapy water, laid over the deposit for a few minutes, softens
fresh sap completely. No solvent, no risk, no stripped wax.

This works often enough that skipping it is a mistake — people reach straight for
chemicals on sap that would have surrendered to a wet towel.

Keep dedicated towels for solvent work. A towel that's absorbed alcohol and dissolved sap
shouldn't go back into your general wash rotation.

## For cured sap, dwell time replaces force

Isopropyl alcohol on a towel, held against the deposit for thirty seconds, then wiped
gently. Repeat as needed.

Apply it to the towel rather than the panel, and work out of direct sun — alcohol
evaporates fast, and a spray that flashes off before it softens anything has done nothing
except strip your wax.

## Never scrape

Cured sap is harder than clear coat. A fingernail, a plastic scraper, a credit card —
all of them put a scratch precisely where the sap was, which is a worse outcome than the
sap.

If it isn't releasing, it needs more dwell time. That is always the answer.

## Reprotect afterward

Alcohol and sap removers strip wax and sealant from every spot they touch. Wash the panel
normally to clear residue, then reapply protection to the treated areas.

Skipping this leaves small patches of bare clear coat scattered across the roof and hood
— usually under the same tree that caused the problem in the first place.

If the mark that's left won't come out and you can feel it, that's etching, and the fix
is the same as for etched water spots — see
[removing water spots](/how-to/how-to-remove-water-spots/).
