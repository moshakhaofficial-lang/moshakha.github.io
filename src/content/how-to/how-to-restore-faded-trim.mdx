Faded black trim is one of the few car care problems where the right product depends
entirely on a test you can do for free in ten seconds.

## Wet it and watch

Splash some plain water on the faded area.

**If the black comes back while it's wet**, the pigment is still in the plastic. The
surface has micro-texture from oxidation that scatters light and reads as grey. A dressing
fills that texture and the colour returns — exactly like the water did, but durably.

**If it stays grey when wet**, the pigment itself has gone. No dressing will restore it,
because there's no colour underneath for the dressing to reveal. That's a dye or trim
restorer job — a product that deposits new pigment rather than revealing old.

Buying the wrong one of those two is the single most common mistake here, and this test
prevents it.

## Why trim fades in the first place

UV breaks down the polymer's surface layer and drives out the plasticisers and carbon
black that give it colour and flexibility. The surface oxidises into a chalky grey.

It's a sun problem primarily — which is why roof rails, mirror caps and the tops of
bumpers go long before the lower sections that live in shadow.

## Prep decides how long it lasts

This is the part people skip, and it matters more than which product you choose.

Faded trim carries oxidised material plus whatever old dressing was applied over it.
Scrub with all-purpose cleaner and a stiff brush until the cloth stops coming away grey —
**dressing applied over oxidation is bonded to a layer that's already flaking off.**

Then wipe down with isopropyl alcohol. Two minutes, and it removes the residue and oils
so the new product bonds to bare plastic.

## Thin coats, buffed

A foam applicator and a thin, even film. Two thin coats an hour apart last considerably
longer than one heavy one.

Once the surface hazes slightly, buff the excess off with a clean microfiber. **Excess
left to cure is what produces the greasy look** and the white streaks that run down the
paint on the first wet drive — the same failure as over-applied dashboard dressing,
covered in
[dashboard dressing and windshield glare](/blog/dashboard-dressing-glare/).

## Mask narrow trim

Window surrounds and thin pillar trim sit tight against paint, and restorers in particular
stain it and are difficult to remove once cured.

Dressings are more forgiving. Restorers are not. Five minutes of tape on anything narrow
is the cheapest insurance in this job.
