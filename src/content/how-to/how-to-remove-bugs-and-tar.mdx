Bugs and tar get grouped together because they show up in the same place — the front
bumper and lower panels — and because both tempt you into the one thing that actually
causes damage: scrubbing.

## Both are bonded, so pressure is the enemy

Neither is loose dirt sitting on the surface. Bug residue dries and adheres. Tar is a
sticky petroleum deposit that has bonded to the clear coat.

Applying force to a hard, bonded deposit means dragging it across the paint. That's how a
careful owner ends up with a scratched bumper while trying to clean it.

**The technique for both is the same in principle: soften first, then remove with almost
no pressure.**

## Bugs: time matters more than technique

Bug residue is acidic. Left through a hot week, it etches into the clear coat, and once
it has etched, removal reveals a dull mark that only polishing levels out.

So the highest-value action isn't a better product — it's removing them sooner. A soaked
microfiber laid over the area for a few minutes rehydrates dried residue far more
effectively and safely than rubbing does. Re-soak and repeat instead of pressing harder.

Skip mesh bug sponges on paint. They work by abrasion, which is the exact mechanism
you're avoiding.

## Tar needs a solvent, not effort

Shampoo won't touch tar — it's petroleum-based and needs a solvent to release. A dedicated
tar remover dissolves it chemically.

Apply out of direct sun, let it dwell for the labeled time, and wipe gently. If it still
resists, reapply and wait longer rather than increasing pressure. Dwell time is doing the
work; your hand isn't supposed to.

## The step almost everyone skips

**Reprotect the treated areas.**

Tar removers strip wax and sealant locally. Every spot you treated is now bare clear
coat, and it's on the lowest, most exposed part of the car — the area that gets hit
hardest by road spray and fresh contamination.

Wash the panels normally to clear solvent residue, then reapply wax or sealant to those
spots. Otherwise the areas you just cleaned will recontaminate faster than the paint
around them.
