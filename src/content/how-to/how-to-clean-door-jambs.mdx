You can wash, decontaminate and wax a car perfectly and it will still read as dirty the
moment someone opens a door. The shuts are what give it away.

## Timing is most of the technique

Clean them **after rinsing, before drying.**

At that moment the jambs are already wet, the grime is soft, and — crucially — you haven't
dried the car yet. Anything that runs out onto a panel gets picked up in the drying pass
you were about to do anyway.

Clean them after the car is dry and you get dirty water tracking down finished panels.
That's why the job has a reputation for being annoying: it's usually done at the wrong
point in the sequence, not because it's difficult.

The wider order-of-operations argument is in
[the two-bucket method](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method/).

## A towel that never touches paint again

Jamb grime is **greasy** in a way panel dirt isn't. It transfers.

A towel used in a door shut and then on a panel leaves a film. Keep one dedicated
low-pile towel for jambs, and launder it separately.

Low pile beats plush here — you're wiping flat painted surfaces and tight corners, not
absorbing volume.

## The seal channel is where the grit hides

The channel the rubber seal sits in collects road film and grit, and a towel bridges
straight across it.

A soft brush lifts it out in seconds. So does a pass around the striker and latch area,
which is the dirtiest part of the aperture and the most visible part when the door's open.

## Hinges last, and leave the grease alone

Hinges and latches carry grease **by design**. You're not trying to remove it.

Wipe the painted area around them, avoid scrubbing the moving parts, and do this section
last so the oil doesn't transfer to everything else you're about to touch.

## Dry the seals, especially in winter

Water sitting in a seal channel runs out onto the paint the next time the door opens —
and in freezing weather it refreezes and glues the door shut.

One quick pass along each seal solves both. That's the same failure mode covered in
[drying a car in freezing weather](/blog/drying-towel-for-winter/).
