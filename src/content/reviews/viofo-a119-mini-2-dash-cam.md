Most dash cam marketing is a resolution number. This one is worth buying for a spec most
listings bury.

## The sensor, not the pixels

Think about when dash cam footage actually matters: dusk, rain, an unlit road, oncoming
headlights. Almost never bright daylight, where any camera copes.

**Those are exactly the conditions that defeat a cheap sensor.** And no resolution fixes
it — a 4K camera with a weak sensor gives you more pixels of the same unreadable smear.

STARVIS 2 is a genuine generational improvement in low-light gathering. That's why this
sits above higher-resolution cameras in [best dash cams](/guides/best-dash-cams/) despite
recording "only" 2K.

## 60fps earns its place

Not for smoothness — for freezing plates.

At 30fps, a plate on a car approaching at combined highway speed can blur across every
frame it appears in. 60fps doubles your chances of catching a clean one. It matters on
fast roads and barely at all in town.

## It's genuinely small

Small enough to sit behind the rear-view mirror and effectively disappear, which has two
benefits: it stays out of your sightline, and it's far less visible to anyone looking into
a parked car.

The absence of a screen is part of how it gets that small. Framing is done through the
phone app once, and then you don't touch it again — which is the normal life of a dash
cam anyway.

## The catch, stated plainly

**It's front-only.**

That's not a flaw in the camera, it's a category decision, and it's the one thing worth
thinking hard about. A front camera records the incident you drove into. It records
nothing when someone hits you from behind, which is a large share of disputed collisions.

If that gap matters more to you than night performance, the
[A129 Plus Duo](/reviews/viofo-a129-plus-duo-dash-cam/) is the other answer at similar
money — two channels, older sensor.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you want the best single-channel footage in poor light, in a unit nobody
notices, and you're content with front coverage only.

**No, if** rear coverage is the thing you actually want. Buy the Duo instead; a better
sensor doesn't help with a collision it can't see.

Either way, pair it with an endurance-rated card — standard cards fail silently under
continuous looping. Sizing is in
[dash cam memory cards](/guides/dash-cam-memory-cards/), and the install decision is in
[how to install a dash cam](/how-to/how-to-install-a-dash-cam/).
