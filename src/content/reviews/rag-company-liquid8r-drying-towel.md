Drying is where most wash-induced swirl marks are actually created, which makes the towel
a more consequential purchase than it looks. The two specifications that decide whether a
drying towel suits your car are rarely the one on the front of the packaging.

## Weave, not GSM

GSM gets printed large and means less than buyers assume. It is grams per square meter —
a density figure. It tells you how much material is there, not how well that material
moves water or how safely it slides over clear coat.

Weave is the structural decision. Twisted loop means the fibers stand up in twisted
columns, and water gets drawn up into those channels and held away from the paint. The
practical effect is that the towel lifts water rather than pushing it. A flat or plush
weave tends to shove a bow wave of water ahead of itself, which is the sensation of
drying a car and never quite finishing.

For bulk water removal — the first pass, where most of the water is — twisted loop is
correct. We go through the full weave comparison in
[microfiber GSM explained](/guides/microfiber-gsm-explained/).

## Size is the actual buying decision

This is the part that decides whether the Liquid8r is right for you, and it has nothing
to do with towel quality.

A 25x36in sheet holds a lot of water. On a full-size SUV, a truck or a van, that is the
point: you can drape it across a roof panel or a hood and pull it, and it will absorb what
is there without saturating. The alternative is a smaller towel that fills up after two
panels and has to be wrung out repeatedly, and wringing a soaking towel is both tedious
and a contamination risk every time you handle it.

On a hatchback or a sedan, the same sheet is awkward. It is heavy when wet, it is hard to
control one-handed around mirrors and badges, and it costs more than you needed to spend.
For those cars the
[Gauntlet at 15x24in](/reviews/rag-company-gauntlet-drying-towel/) is the better tool, and
that review makes the case for controllability over capacity.

So the honest framing: this is not a better towel than the Gauntlet. It is a towel sized
for a different vehicle.

## Where it falls short

Drying time between uses is the real ownership cost. A 1100 GSM twisted loop sheet holds
a lot of water and takes a long time to give it back. You need somewhere to hang it fully
extended — draped over a rail, not folded in a bucket. A towel put away damp develops
mildew in the pile, and once it smells, that smell transfers to the car.

Glass is the other limitation. There is a lot of fiber surface here and some of it ends up
on your windows. Keep a separate waffle weave towel for glass.

## Use a drying aid

Worth doing regardless of which towel you buy. A light mist of drying aid or quick
detailer as you dry adds lubrication, so the towel glides instead of grabbing, and it
reduces water spotting on the panels you have not reached yet.

This matters more on a large vehicle precisely because drying takes longer — the first
panels you washed have had time to start drying on their own, which is how water spots
form. [Drying aids explained](/blog/drying-aid-explained/) covers what they do.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you drive a full-size SUV, a truck or a van, or you regularly dry two cars in
a session. The capacity is the feature and it removes the wringing cycle entirely.

**No, if** you drive something hatchback or sedan sized — you will fight the towel for no
benefit. And keep it out of the same wash load as cotton, or the pile clogs and the
absorbency goes;
[microfiber care 101](/blog/microfibre-care-101/) has the laundry rules.
