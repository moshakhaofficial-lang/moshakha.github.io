Interior cleaners are a category where the product does less of the work than people
expect. The technique and the towel matter more. What genuinely separates one bottle
from another is what it leaves behind.

## The glare problem

This is the thing worth understanding before buying any interior product.

A dashboard is a large, angled surface sitting directly below the windshield. Anything
applied to it that leaves a glossy film turns it into a mirror aimed at the glass. In
overcast conditions you will never notice. On a low sun — early morning commute, late
afternoon drive west — that reflection lands in your sightline and the windshield becomes
noticeably harder to see through.

This is why detailers who care about driving rather than photography use matte interior
products. Total Interior dries matte. That single property is more valuable than any
cleaning-performance difference between it and its competitors, and it is the reason to
pick it over a gloss dressing that costs the same.

[why dashboard dressings cause glare](/blog/dashboard-dressing-glare/).

## What "all-surface" actually covers

The claim holds for hard surfaces: dashboard, door cards, center console, most plastic
and vinyl trim, and the back of the instrument binnacle. One bottle and one towel will
do a full interior wipe-down in ten minutes.

Where the claim thins out:

- **Leather seats.** It will not harm coated leather, but it is not conditioning
  anything. That is a different product doing a different job.
- **Screens.** Keep it away from them entirely.
- **Fabric and carpet.** Wrong product. You need an upholstery cleaner and extraction.
- **Interior glass.** It will leave a film. Glass wants a dedicated ammonia-free cleaner.

So "total" means "most of the hard surfaces", which is still the majority of what you
touch in a routine interior clean.

## Where it falls short

It is a maintenance product, not a restoration one. On a dashboard that has gone sticky,
or a console with months of built-up skin oil and spilled coffee, one pass will lift the
loose material and leave the embedded layer behind. You need a second pass, and sometimes
an all-purpose cleaner diluted properly before you get to this.

That distinction between a maintenance spray and a genuine cleaner is worth having
clear in your head —
[interior detailer vs all-purpose cleaner](/blog/interior-detailer-vs-all-purpose-cleaner/)
covers which one your interior actually needs.

The fragrance is also strong. In a car left closed in the sun after application it is
noticeable for a day or two. If you are sensitive to that, apply with the doors open and
give it longer to flash off than feels necessary.

## Using it without streaking

Spray onto the towel, not onto the panel. This matters for two reasons: overspray
reaches glass and screens, and a panel sprayed directly gets more product than it needs,
which is what causes the streaking people then blame on the cleaner.

Two towels is the other half of it. One damp with product to clean, one dry to buff
behind it. A single towel that is progressively wetter and dirtier redistributes what it
just picked up, which is the actual cause of most "this product streaks" complaints.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you want one bottle that handles routine interior maintenance without
creating a glare problem. That is a narrow claim and it delivers on it.

**No, if** you are trying to rescue a badly neglected interior — start with an
all-purpose cleaner and a brush, then use something like this to maintain it afterwards.
