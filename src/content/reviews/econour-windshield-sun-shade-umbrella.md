Sun shades are sold on heat-reduction percentages that no standard test produces. The spec
that actually decides the outcome isn't printed on any of them.

## The specification nobody lists: whether you'll use it

An accordion shade is cheap, durable and stores flat. A significant number of people stop
using one, because folding it in a hot parking lot with an armful of shopping is genuinely
annoying.

**A shade in the trunk does nothing.** The most effective shade is the one that's actually
deployed, every time, without thinking about it.

This opens and collapses in seconds, one-handed, and goes into a door pocket as a slim
rod. If you've owned a shade before and stopped using it, that's the variable worth
optimizing for — and it's why this leads our ranking in
[best windshield sun shades](/guides/best-windshield-sun-shades/) despite having a
mechanism that can eventually fail.

## What a shade genuinely does

It blocks direct sunlight from landing on the dashboard, steering wheel and front seats.

Those surfaces reach temperatures far above cabin air, which is why a steering wheel can
be painful to hold while the air merely feels hot. **The cabin air difference is smaller
than the surface temperature difference.**

A shaded car is still hot when you get in. The dash and wheel just aren't scorching, and
the interior plastics aren't taking UV damage while you're at work. That's a worthwhile
outcome — it just isn't the outcome the percentage claims imply.

## Coverage beats reflectivity

The one specification you can verify yourself.

At 58 x 32 inches this covers most sedans and mid-size SUVs. **Measure your own
windshield**, because size labels mean nothing consistent between brands, and a shade
leaving four inches of glass uncovered along the top is admitting sunlight straight onto
the dash regardless of what the covered portion is made of.

Full-size trucks and large SUVs want the larger double-layer option instead.

## Where it's weaker

**The rib mechanism is the wear point.** Spring ribs and the hinge take stress every time
it opens and closes, and that's where failures appear — not in the fabric. An accordion
shade has nothing to break, which is the honest argument for the older design.

**Steeply raked windshields** suit it less well. The ribs want a reasonably upright plane;
a very shallow screen is where a folding shade still fits more neatly.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you've previously owned a sun shade and stopped bothering with it. This is the
one that fixes that, and the fix is worth more than any material claim.

**No, if** your windshield is unusually steeply raked, or you want something with no
mechanism at all to fail.

One related thing worth knowing if you're buying this because the dashboard is baking: a
**glossy** dashboard dressing makes the other summer problem worse, reflecting low sun up
into the windshield as a haze. Matte protectant avoids it —
[dashboard dressing and windshield glare](/blog/dashboard-dressing-glare/).
