The Gauntlet is the towel most detailing forums land on when someone asks what to buy
first, and it costs roughly three times what a supermarket microfibre towel does. That
gap is worth interrogating.

## What you're actually paying for

Two things, and neither is the brand name.

**The blend ratio.** Most budget microfibre is 80/20 polyester to polyamide. The
Gauntlet is 70/30. Polyamide is the component that actually absorbs, and it's the
expensive one — which is why cheap towels use less of it. The practical effect is that
this 900 GSM towel out-absorbs many 1100+ GSM budget towels, because density without
polyamide content is just weight.

**The size decision.** At 15x24in this is deliberately not the biggest towel available,
and that is the point. It's large enough to take a door or a quarter panel in one pass,
and small enough that you can control it with one hand while it's soaked. Larger towels
sound better on a spec sheet and are worse to actually use on a normal car.

## Where it falls down

On a full-size SUV or a truck it simply runs out of capacity. You'll be wringing it out
three or four times, and a wrung towel is never as effective as a dry one. That isn't a
flaw so much as a mismatch — The Rag Company sells the Liquid8r at 1100 GSM and 25x36in
for exactly this case, and that's the one to buy for a large vehicle.

It's also the wrong towel for glass. At 900 GSM the pile is too deep to make consistent
contact with a perfectly smooth surface, so it smears the final film of moisture rather
than lifting it. Keep a low-pile towel for windows; this is true of every drying towel,
not a specific failing here.

## Durability

The suede edge matters more than it sounds. Many towels use a stitched polyester
overlock border that is harder than clear coat and stiffens with age — a genuine marring
risk on soft paint. A suede or edgeless border removes that risk entirely.

Reported longevity is good past 50 wash cycles, though this depends far more on
laundering than on the towel. Fabric softener will ruin this towel just as fast as it
ruins a cheap one — see our [microfibre care guide](/blog/microfibre-care-101/).

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you have a sedan, hatchback or small crossover, you wash it regularly, and
you want one towel that does the job properly for years.

**No, if** you drive something large — get the Liquid8r instead — or if you wash your car
twice a year, in which case a budget twisted loop towel is perfectly adequate and our
[drying towel guide](/guides/best-car-drying-towels/) covers those too.
