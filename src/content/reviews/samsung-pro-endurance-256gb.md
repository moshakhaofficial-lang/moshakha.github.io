A dash cam is only as reliable as the card in it, and the card is the part people spend
the least thought on. There are two decisions here: the speed rating, and the capacity.
The second one depends entirely on how many channels you are running.

## Why two channels changes the maths

A dash cam records in a loop. It fills the card, then starts overwriting the oldest
footage. The useful question is how far back that loop reaches — your retention window.

A front-only camera writes one video stream. Add a rear channel and you are writing two,
so roughly twice the data per minute, so the loop wraps in roughly half the time.

This is the specific failure that catches people. You had a front camera with a 128GB card
and several hours of retention, which was plenty. You add a rear camera, keep the same
card, and now your window is halved — and it may now be shorter than the realistic gap
between something happening and you remembering to pull the footage.

That gap is often days. Someone keys your car in a parking lot on Friday and you notice
on Monday. If the loop only holds a few hours, the footage is gone.

256GB on a dual-channel setup restores a sensible window. That is the whole argument for
this card over a smaller one, and
[the memory card guide](/guides/dash-cam-memory-cards/) works through the sizing.

## The rating trap

This is the most important practical point about this specific product.

Samsung has sold PRO Endurance cards at different speed classes under very similar naming,
and the older U1-rated versions are still in circulation. U1 guarantees 10MB/s sustained
write. U3 and V30 guarantee 30MB/s.

For a 2K or 4K camera, especially a dual-channel one, 10MB/s is not enough headroom. A
card that cannot sustain the write rate drops frames, produces corrupt files, or stops
recording — and a dash cam that has silently stopped recording is worse than no dash cam,
because you are relying on it.

So: read the rating on the listing. Look for U3 and V30. Do not assume the product family
name tells you what you are getting, and treat a price that looks too good as a reason to
check rather than a bargain.

## Endurance and speed are different claims

Worth separating, because the marketing blends them.

**Speed** (U3, V30) is about whether the card can keep up with the camera writing to it
right now.

**Endurance** is about how many times it can be overwritten before the cells wear out. A
dash cam overwrites continuously, forever, which is a workload ordinary cards are not
designed for — a standard card in a dash cam typically fails within months.

You need both. A fast card with ordinary endurance dies early; a high-endurance card that
is too slow drops frames. The PRO Endurance line is built for the overwrite cycle, and
the U3/V30 version has the speed as well.

Our [SanDisk High Endurance review](/reviews/sandisk-high-endurance-128gb/) covers the
same reasoning for a single-channel install, where 128GB is the sensible size.

## When this is too much card

Be honest about your setup. If you are running one 1080p channel, this is more capacity
than the job needs and a 128GB high-endurance card is the better value.

The capacity argument here is specifically about dual-channel, 2K and 4K installs — the
configurations where write volume is high enough that a smaller card gives you a retention
window too short to be useful.

## Treat it as a consumable

High-endurance does not mean permanent. The card is being overwritten continuously, and it
will eventually wear out.

Two habits: format it in the camera every few months, because filesystem errors accumulate
and the camera's own format is the one that matters. And replace it roughly annually
rather than waiting for failure — the moment you discover a dead card is almost always
the moment you needed it.

Most cameras warn about card errors and most people ignore the warning. Do not.
[What to do with dash cam footage after an incident](/blog/dash-cam-footage-after-an-incident/)
is only useful if the footage exists.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you are running front and rear channels, or a 2K or 4K camera, and you want a
retention window measured in days rather than hours. Verify U3 and V30 on the listing.

**No, if** you have a single 1080p channel — buy a 128GB high-endurance card and spend the
difference on [a better camera](/guides/best-dash-cams/).
