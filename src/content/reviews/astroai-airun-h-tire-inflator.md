Tire inflators are sold on maximum PSI, which is close to irrelevant for a car. The spec
that actually changes the experience is on the back of the box.

## Auto shut-off is the whole product

Set the target pressure. Walk away. It stops.

That sounds minor and it isn't, because the alternative is standing over a running
compressor watching a gauge — and **built-in gauges read pressure at the compressor rather
than in the tire**, so they read slightly high. Combine that with a loud motor and a
distracted person and over-inflation is the normal outcome.

Over-inflation isn't harmless. It reduces the tire's contact patch, hurts wet grip, and
wears out the center of the tread. The correct target pressure, and why it isn't the
number on the sidewall, is in
[how to check tire pressure](/how-to/how-to-check-tire-pressure/).

## Corded is the right call here

Counterintuitive, because cordless sounds better.

A cordless inflator is more pleasant to use and will be flat on the day you need it,
because it's been sitting in the trunk unused for eight months. **A corded unit has no
charge state to manage.** For something whose entire job is to work on an unplanned day,
that's the more important property.

Cordless earns its place in one specific case: inflating something away from the car — a
bike, a trailer, or a car whose battery is already dead. That's a genuine use, and it's
why the range includes one.

## Check your 12V socket first

This is the thing to verify before ordering, not after.

**Many modern cars cut power to the 12V socket when the ignition is off.** If yours does,
you'd have to run the engine to use this — unpleasant generally, and genuinely unsafe in a
closed garage.

It takes ten seconds to test: key out, plug something in, see if it lights up. Full
explanation in
[does your car's 12V socket stay on with the ignition off](/blog/does-12v-socket-stay-on-with-ignition-off/).

## What it isn't

**It's slow on a fully flat tire.** This is a compact 12V compressor, not workshop
equipment. Topping up a few PSI is quick; filling from zero takes real patience.

Judge it as a maintenance tool that also covers an emergency, rather than as recovery
equipment. That's the honest framing, and it's still the right thing to own.

**It's loud.** All of them in this class are.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** your 12V socket stays live, and you want something that makes monthly pressure
checks realistic rather than a trip to a garage forecourt.

**No, if** your socket cuts with the ignition — buy the cordless or dual-power version
instead, both in [best tire inflators](/guides/best-tire-inflators/).

Paired with a jump starter, it covers the two failures that actually strand people — the
argument for that pairing is in
