Power stations are sold on watt-hours. The number that actually decides whether this is
still useful in five years is printed much smaller.

## LiFePO4 is the spec that matters

**Lithium iron phosphate tolerates substantially more charge cycles than the lithium-ion
packs older models use**, and it copes better with heat — which matters for anything that
lives in a car.

Two units with identical 288Wh ratings can be very different products once you've cycled
them a few hundred times. One holds most of its capacity; the other doesn't.

That's the whole reason this sits above the older
[Explorer 300](/guides/best-portable-power-stations/) in our ranking despite near-identical
capacity. For a unit used twice a year the difference barely registers. For one you
actually use, it decides the lifespan.

## The watt-hour figure is a ceiling

288Wh is not what you'll get out of it.

Anything drawn through the AC outlets passes through an **inverter**, and that conversion
loses a meaningful share as heat. Then the device you plug in does its own conversion.

**Charging over USB-C straight from DC skips one of those conversions** and lands much
closer to the rated number. That's the single most useful habit with one of these, and
this unit's USB-C PD input and output is what makes it practical.

## What 300W refuses

This is the limit worth understanding before buying, because it's absolute rather than
gradual.

**Fine:** laptops, phones, tablets, camera batteries, lights, many CPAP machines.

**Refuses:** kettles, hair dryers, induction hobs, compressors, most power tools.

Anything with a motor or a heating element draws a startup surge far above its running
wattage. A 300W unit doesn't struggle with those — it declines. If you need appliances,
that's a different and much heavier class of product.

## Light enough to get used

Easy to undervalue. A power station that's awkward to carry gets left in the garage, and a
unit in the garage has zero capacity when you want it.

This is light enough to take without planning around it, which in practice is worth more
than the extra capacity of something you leave behind.

## It is not a jump starter

Worth stating because people assume otherwise. A jump starter delivers a huge current
burst for seconds; this delivers moderate power for hours. Attempting the former with the
latter can damage it.

If roadside failure is the thing you're covering, that's a
[jump starter](/guides/best-jump-starters/), and the broader argument about what's worth
carrying is in [car emergency kits](/guides/best-car-emergency-kit/).

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you want to keep phones, a laptop and camera gear running through a weekend,
in something you'll actually carry, and you want the battery to still be good years from
now.

**No, if** you need to run anything with a motor or a heating element — that's a 1000Wh,
1500W+ purchase and a two-hands item.
