The memory card is the component most likely to fail in a dash cam setup, and the only one
that fails without telling you.

## The failure mode is the whole argument

An ordinary microSD card is built for the workload a phone or camera gives it — write some
files, read them back, occasionally delete.

A dash cam does something fundamentally different. It writes continuously, fills the card,
overwrites the oldest footage, and keeps going, every minute the engine runs.

That cycle wears out flash memory. And when a worn card starts failing, **the camera
usually doesn't tell you.** The recording light stays on. Files still appear. You find out
when you go looking for a specific clip after the incident that made you want it.

There's no warning stage. That's the entire reason to spend more here than feels
proportionate.

## Endurance and speed are different claims

Worth separating, because the packaging mixes them.

**Endurance** is how much total data the card can absorb before wearing out. That's what
determines whether it survives two years of looping.

**V30** guarantees 30MB/s *sustained* write. That's what determines whether frames get
dropped while recording.

You want both, and this card has both. A fast card that isn't endurance-rated wears out;
an endurance card that's only U1-rated can't keep up with 2K. The ratings are printed on
the card itself, which is more reliable than the listing title — a trap covered in
[dash cam memory cards](/guides/dash-cam-memory-cards/).

## Why 128GB, specifically

It's the size that matches a single-channel camera without paying for retention you'll
never look at.

Where it stops being enough:

| Setup | This card? |
|---|---|
| Single 1080p or 2K channel | Yes — the sweet spot |
| Front + rear channels | Size up to 256GB |
| Parking mode enabled | Size up to 256–512GB |

Parking mode is the one that changes the maths most, because the camera writes while
you're not there. What that actually requires is in
[how to install a dash cam](/how-to/how-to-install-a-dash-cam/).

## Treat it as a consumable

It's wearing out from the day you fit it. Two habits cost nothing:

- **Format in the camera** every month or two, not on a computer. The camera writes the
  file layout it expects, and formatting clears the fragmentation that builds under
  constant looping.
- **Replace it every year or two**, or sooner if the camera reports errors. A card that
  needs formatting twice is telling you something.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you run a single-channel dash cam and want to stop thinking about the card.
It's the default for good reason.

**No, if** you're running two channels or parking mode — not because it's wrong, but
because you'll be fighting a short loop. Buy the 256GB or 512GB version of the same card
instead.
