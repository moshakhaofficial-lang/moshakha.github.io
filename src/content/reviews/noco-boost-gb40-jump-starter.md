The Boost GB40 is our top overall pick in [best jump starters](/guides/best-jump-starters/),
and it gets there with the lowest peak-amp claim of any pack on that list. That's worth
explaining, because on paper it looks like the weakest option.

## The spec that doesn't matter, and the one that does

Every jump-starter listing leads with a peak-amp number, and there's no consistent
standard for how brands measure it. One manufacturer's 4000A and another's 1000A can be
measured under completely different conditions — comparing them across brands tells you
almost nothing.

What actually determines whether a jump starter is safe to use is the protection
circuitry, and this is where the GB40 earns its rank.

**Spark-proof connection** means the pack delivers no current until it detects a correct
clamp connection. **Reverse-polarity protection** means clamping backwards does nothing,
rather than damaging the car's electronics, the pack, or the battery. Jump starting is
the one routine car task with a genuine injury risk — a discharged lead-acid battery
vents hydrogen, and a spark at the terminal is the ignition source. Protection
circuitry is what turns that from a real hazard into a non-event.

Cheap packs without this protection still start cars. They just remove the margin for
error made in the dark, in the rain, by someone who does this once every three years.

## Where it actually falls short

**It won't crank a large diesel.** 1000A peak is comfortable for petrol engines up to
roughly 6.0L. Diesels need substantially more cranking current because of higher
compression, and this is a genuine limit, not marketing caution — buy a GB50 or GB70
instead if that's your vehicle.

**No air compressor.** Some competitors bundle one in. NOCO doesn't, and on balance
that's the right call: combination units compromise both functions and are heavier, so
they get left at home. A dedicated GB40 plus a corded inflator like the one in our
[car emergency kit guide](/guides/best-car-emergency-kit/) covers both failure modes
without either one being a compromise.

**It costs more than no-name packs with bigger claimed numbers.** That premium buys the
protection circuitry above, plus clamps and build quality that are visibly better than
the budget tier. Whether that's worth it depends on how much you value not thinking
about it again for years.

## The failure that isn't the product's fault

A jump starter that isn't charged is a brick, and lithium packs self-discharge slowly
enough that a forgotten one goes flat over a year or two sitting in a boot. Recharge the
GB40 every three to six months on a calendar reminder — this is the single most common
reason jump starters disappoint their owners, and it has nothing to do with build
quality.

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** your car is a small-to-mid petrol engine and you want protection circuitry
that removes the actual risk from jump starting, in a pack small enough to actually keep
in the car.

**No, if** you drive a diesel or a large-capacity petrol engine — size up to a GB50 or
GB70 — or if you want a combination jump-starter/inflator and are willing to accept the
compromises that comes with.
