Dash cams compete on resolution because it's the number that prints well. This one earns
its place on a different spec entirely.

## The second channel is the product

A front-only dash cam records the incident you drove into. It records nothing at all when
someone goes into the back of you — which is a large share of contested collisions, and
the ones where fault is most often disputed.

**2K front plus 1080p rear covers that.** You are not buying more pixels; you are buying
coverage of a failure the sharpest single camera physically cannot see.

That framing matters when comparing against a 4K single-channel camera at similar money.
The 4K unit gives you more detail of the thing you were already recording. This gives you
footage of the thing you weren't.

## 2K front is enough

Enough to read a plate at sensible distance, which is the job.

Resolution beyond that mostly fills memory cards faster and generates more heat on a
windshield. What actually determines whether a plate is legible is sensor quality and
light — and the A129 Plus is competent rather than exceptional there, which is the honest
trade for having two channels at this price. If night performance on a single channel
matters more to you, the [A119 Mini 2](/reviews/viofo-a119-mini-2-dash-cam/) has the newer
sensor.

## Supercapacitor, not battery

Non-negotiable in a dash cam, and this has one.

A camera stuck to a windshield in summer reaches temperatures that degrade lithium
batteries quickly, with a small but real swelling risk. A supercapacitor only needs to
hold enough charge to close the current file cleanly when power drops, and it tolerates
heat far better. Every pick in [best dash cams](/guides/best-dash-cams/) uses one for this
reason.

## Where the cost actually lands

Not the price — the install.

Running cable to the tailgate means tucking it along the headliner, down a rear pillar,
and usually through a rubber grommet into the tailgate itself. It is within reach for most
people with a trim tool, and it is an afternoon rather than ten minutes.

Then, if you want parking mode, there's hardwiring on top — the 12V socket won't do it,
because most cars cut that with the ignition. The full decision is in
[how to install a dash cam](/how-to/how-to-install-a-dash-cam/).

## Pair it with the right card

Two channels roughly double the write rate, which halves your loop length.

A standard photography card will fail under continuous overwriting, and it fails silently
— the camera keeps appearing to record while producing unreadable files. Size up and buy
endurance-rated: [dash cam memory cards](/guides/dash-cam-memory-cards/).

## Should you buy it?

**Yes, if** you want the rear coverage and you're willing to spend an afternoon on the
install. That's the honest summary — the hardware is good and the labour is real.

**No, if** you want something fitted in ten minutes, or your priority is the best possible
single-channel night footage rather than two channels of good footage.

And whichever you fit, know what to do in the first minutes after an incident — the loop
is already overwriting it. That's in
[saving dash cam footage after an incident](/blog/dash-cam-footage-after-an-incident/).
