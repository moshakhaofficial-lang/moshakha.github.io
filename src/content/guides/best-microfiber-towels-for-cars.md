Search for the best microfibre towel and you'll get roundups that rank a glass towel
against a drying towel as though they're alternatives. They aren't. They're different
tools, and the honest answer to "which is best" is **"for what?"**

This guide is organised by job. Each pick is the right tool for one task and the wrong
tool for the others.

## The five jobs

Every car cleaning task falls into one of these, and each wants a different towel:

1. **Washing** — media in contact with paint the longest. Highest risk of swirls.
2. **Drying panels** — second highest risk. Wants high GSM and twisted loop.
3. **Glass** — wants the *opposite*: low pile, tight weave.
4. **Wax and sealant removal** — wants deep plush pile to lift cured residue.
5. **Wheels** — wants a dedicated tool that never touches paint.

Using one towel for all five is the most common mistake in home detailing, and it's why
people end up with streaky glass and swirled paint despite buying good products.

## How we chose

Specification analysis — weave, blend ratio, pile depth and edge construction — combined
with aggregated long-term owner feedback, weighted toward how each holds up after 20+
wash cycles. We haven't lab-tested these and don't claim to have. No brand paid for a
place here.

## The rule that saves your paint

**Never let a towel move up the contamination ladder.**

Wheels → lower panels → upper panels → glass. A towel can always move *down* that list
after laundering. It can never move back up. A towel that has cleaned a wheel carries
metallic brake dust in its pile permanently as far as your paint is concerned.

Colour-coding is the easiest way to enforce this: pick a colour per job and never
deviate. The colour does nothing on its own — the system does.
