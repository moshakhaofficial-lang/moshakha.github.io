A pressure washer is genuinely useful for washing a car, and the specification everyone
buys on is the one most likely to damage the paint.

## PSI is the wrong number to optimise

Listings lead with PSI because it's a big number that sounds like capability. For car
washing it is closer to a risk rating.

Water at high pressure through a narrow nozzle will lift failing clear coat, force water
past trim seals, blast at stone chips and get underneath paint edges, and strip
decals. None of this requires an unusual machine — a standard domestic unit with a
narrow tip fitted will do it.

**Roughly 1200–1900 PSI at the surface is the sensible range for paint.** Note "at the
surface": a 2200 PSI machine with a wide fan held a foot away is gentler than a 1600 PSI
machine with a narrow tip pressed close.

**How you use it matters more than what it's rated at.**

## GPM is the number that actually helps

Gallons per minute is flow rate, and flow is what carries loosened dirt off the panel.

Pressure dislodges; flow removes. For rinsing a car, higher flow at moderate pressure
beats high pressure at low flow — which is why professional detailers generally aren't
using the most powerful machines on the shelf.

## Nozzles: the only ones you should own for a car

| Nozzle | Angle | Use on a car |
|---|---|---|
| Red | 0° | **Never.** Will damage paint immediately |
| Yellow | 15° | Wheel arches and stubborn wheel grime only |
| Green | 25° | Lower panels, sills — with care |
| White | 40° | **The car nozzle.** Rinsing and general washing |
| Black | 65° / soap | Applying detergent |
| Turbo | rotating 0° | **Never on a vehicle** |

If you own a pressure washer and use it on your car, the white 40° tip should be the one
that lives on the wand.

## The foam cannon is the point

For car washing specifically, this is the real reason to own the machine.

Pre-foaming covers the car in detergent that loosens and lifts grit **before anything
touches the paint**. Since almost all swirl marks come from dragging grit across a
panel, removing grit without contact is the single most effective thing you can do.

It doesn't replace the contact wash — bonded road film still needs a mitt. It
substantially reduces how much grit that mitt encounters, which is exactly the goal of
[the two-bucket method](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method).

Specification analysis — motor rating, flow rate, nozzle set, and included accessories

## Where we'd draw a line

We would not put a **powered brush of any kind on paint**. Rotary and utility brushes are
fine on wheels, arches and sills — surfaces where marring doesn't matter and dirt is
heaviest — but a wash mitt is the correct tool for painted panels, and nothing here
changes that.

Anything used on wheels never returns to paint. Brake dust is metallic and scratches on
contact.
