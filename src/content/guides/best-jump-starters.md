A jump starter is insurance. The right way to buy it is to work out what failure you're
covering, then buy the cheapest thing that covers it properly — not the biggest number
on a box.

## Peak amps are close to meaningless

This is the number every listing leads with, and it's the least useful spec in the
category.

There is **no consistent standard** for how brands measure "peak amps". One
manufacturer's 4000A and another's 2000A may be measured under completely different
conditions for wildly different durations. Comparing them across brands tells you almost
nothing.

What actually matters:

- **Engine size and fuel type.** Diesels need substantially more cranking current than
  petrol engines of the same capacity, because of higher compression.
- **Cold performance.** Batteries fail on cold mornings. That's when the pack works
  hardest, and lithium packs also deliver less when cold.
- **Protection circuitry.** See below.
- **Whether it's charged.** By a wide margin the most important factor, and entirely
  down to you.

Use peak amps as a rough tier — small petrol, large petrol, diesel — and ignore the
precision the numbers imply.

## The safety features are the product

Jump starting is the one routine car maintenance task with a genuine injury risk. A
discharged lead-acid battery vents hydrogen. A spark at the terminal is the ignition
source. That is a real, if uncommon, way people get hurt.

**Spark-proof connection** means the pack delivers no current until it detects a correct
connection. **Reverse-polarity protection** means clamping backwards does nothing rather
than damaging the car, the pack, or the battery.

Cheap packs without these still work. They also remove the margin for a mistake made in
the dark, in the rain, by someone who does this once every three years.

This is what the NOCO premium buys, and it's the reason it ranks first despite the
lowest amp figure here.

## How we chose

Specification analysis — protection circuitry, capacity, charge retention, clamp and
cable quality — plus aggregated long-term owner feedback, weighted toward cold-weather
performance and charge retention after long storage. No lab testing, no paid placement.

## The failure nobody plans for

**A jump starter you didn't charge is a brick.**

Lithium packs self-discharge slowly. A pack put in the boot two years ago and forgotten
will be flat on the morning you need it, and you'll conclude the product was bad. It
wasn't.

Recharge every three to six months. Set a recurring reminder. This is why pick three's
dual-purpose design is genuinely useful — a pack you also use as a power bank gets
charged as a side effect of normal life.

## Sizing, roughly

| Vehicle | Look for |
|---|---|
| Small–mid petrol (up to ~3.0L) | 500–1000A peak class |
| Large petrol (up to ~6.0L) | 1000A peak class |
| Diesel up to ~3.0L | 1000–2000A peak class |
| Large diesel (up to ~10L) | 3000A+ peak class |

Buying more headroom than you need costs weight and money but does no harm. Buying less
means it won't crank on the cold morning it's needed.
