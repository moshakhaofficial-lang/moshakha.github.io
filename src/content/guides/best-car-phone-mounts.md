Phone mounts are a category where the brand matters far less than the **mounting
method**, and the right method is decided by your specific car rather than by reviews.

## The three mounting points, and where each fails

**Air vent.** Keeps the phone high and near your sightline, needs no adhesive, and works
on any dashboard finish. It also puts your phone directly in the path of your climate
control, which is a real problem — see below.

**Dashboard.** Most flexible positioning, and the least reliable attachment. Suction
needs a smooth non-porous surface, and almost every modern dashboard is textured. That
mismatch is why so many suction mounts end up in the footwell.

**Windscreen.** The only surface where suction genuinely works well, because it's smooth
glass. It's also the most likely to be legally restricted — several US states limit
where on the screen you may mount anything at all.

## The vent mount heat problem

This is the thing worth knowing before you buy the most popular type of mount.

A vent mount places your phone in the direct path of the air your climate control is
pushing. Run the heater in winter and you're blowing hot air over a lithium battery for
the length of your commute, every day. **Sustained heat permanently degrades battery
capacity** — it does not recover.

Air conditioning creates the opposite issue: cold air on a warm phone produces
condensation.

None of this makes vent mounts unusable. It does mean not aiming that particular vent at
the phone while running climate control hard, which is easy once you know.

## Magnetic is worth it if your phone supports it

The practical failure of clamp mounts isn't strength — it's that seating a phone in a
clamp needs two hands and a moment of attention. That moment tends to arrive while
you're moving.

A magnetic mount is genuinely one-handed. That is a safety argument as much as a
convenience one.

The caveat: magnetic hold falls off sharply with distance. A thick or textured case can
weaken it enough for the phone to drop over a pothole. Check case compatibility rather
than assuming.

## How we chose

Specification analysis — clamp mechanism and materials, magnet strength claims, base
type and arm length — plus aggregated long-term owner feedback with attention to
suction failure in heat and cold, and clip breakage. No lab testing. No paid placement.

## Before you fit anything

**Clean the surface properly.** Both suction cups and adhesive pads fail on the oily
plasticiser film that builds up on interior glass and dashboards. Most "the mount won't
stick" complaints are a cleaning problem, not a product problem.

An alcohol-based interior cleaner or a proper glass cleaner, then let it dry fully. Our
[interior cleaning guide](/guides/best-car-interior-cleaning-products/) covers what's
safe on which surface.

If you're also fitting a dash cam, do both at the same time — same preparation, same
awkward reach behind the mirror. Our [dash cam guide](/guides/best-dash-cams/) covers
that side.
