Tyre dressing is the highest-visibility, lowest-cost step in detailing. It is also the
one people most reliably get wrong, in two specific ways.

## Failure one: dressing over dirt

This is why tyres go brown.

Old dressing does not simply wear away. It builds up, oxidises, and turns a dull brown
that shows through everything applied on top of it. Each new coat traps another layer.
People blame the product and buy a different one, which then browns the same way.

**The fix is a cleaning step, not a better dressing.** Scrub the sidewall with a stiff
brush and a dedicated tyre cleaner until the rubber comes back to a flat, dark grey.
That is what clean rubber looks like — the deep black is the dressing, not the tyre.

Do this once properly and everything afterwards works better.

## Failure two: sling

Sling is dressing flung off the rotating sidewall onto your paint, in a fine speckled
arc down the side of the car. It happens for one reason: **too much product, still wet,
when you drove off.**

Three habits eliminate it:

1. **Apply to a pad, never spray onto the tyre.** Overspray reaches brake discs and
   paint before you've even started.
2. **Wipe the sidewall after applying.** Take the excess off with a cloth. What remains
   is what bonds.
3. **Let it flash.** Give it a few minutes before moving the car.

Gel slings less than spray because it is thicker and less inclined to migrate. That is
most of why it ranks first here.

## How we chose

Specification analysis — carrier type, water- versus petroleum-based formulation, and
stated surface compatibility — plus aggregated long-term owner feedback, weighted
toward how each product behaves through rain and repeated washing. No lab testing, and
no brand paid for a place here.

## Why only three picks

Because these are the three worth recommending. Tyre dressing is a category where most
products do roughly the same thing, and a ten-item ranking would be padding designed to
carry more affiliate links rather than to help you choose.

You need a cleaner and one dressing. That's the whole category.

## The order that works

1. **Wheels and tyres first**, before touching paint —
   [why that matters](/blog/wheel-rim-care)
2. Scrub sidewalls with tyre cleaner and a stiff brush
3. Rinse thoroughly and let the tyre dry
4. Apply dressing to an applicator pad, work it into the sidewall
5. **Wipe off the excess**
6. Let it flash before driving
7. Then [wash the car](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method)

Dressing a wet tyre is wasted product — it will not bond, and it will sling.
