  - q: "What order should I clean a car interior in?"
    a: "Dry before wet, top to bottom, glass last. Vacuum and dust from the headliner downward first, then hard surfaces, then fabric or leather, then interior glass. Cleaning glass first guarantees you will have to do it twice, because everything afterwards mists or splashes it."
  - q: "Why does my dashboard cause windscreen glare?"
    a: "Silicone gloss dressings. They look impressive for a day, then reflect into the glass on low sun and become a genuine visibility problem, while attracting dust so the surface looks worse within a week. A matte interior cleaner avoids the whole issue."
  - q: "Do leather conditioners actually feed the leather?"
    a: "Not on most modern cars. Almost all automotive leather is heavily coated with a polyurethane finish, so a conditioner is working on that coating rather than on the hide beneath. It still improves appearance and slows wear on the coating, but the 'feeding' language is largely marketing."

Microfibre is where we started, because it is the category where marketing drifts
furthest from reality. Interiors are the category where getting it wrong is
**permanent**.

A towel used badly leaves a streak you can wipe away. An ammonia cleaner on a
UV-coated dashboard, or a silicone dressing baked into vinyl over a summer, is not
reversible. So this guide is organised by surface rather than by ranking — these
products are not competing with each other.

## How we chose

Specification analysis — pH, ammonia and silicone content, and material compatibility
with automotive plastics, coated leather and TPE — combined with aggregated long-term
owner feedback. We have not lab-tested these and don't claim to have. No brand paid for
placement.

## The five surfaces

Every interior job falls into one of these, and each wants different chemistry:

| Surface | Wants | Never use |
|---|---|---|
| **Carpet + upholstery** | Dry extraction first, then minimal moisture | Soaking — the underlay traps it |
| **Hard trim, dash, console** | Matte, UV-safe, low-silicone cleaner | Gloss dressings, household sprays |
| **Coated leather** | pH-balanced cleaner, then conditioner | Alkaline cleaners, saddle soap |
| **Interior glass** | Ammonia-free, low-pile towel | Anything ammonia-based near tint |
| **Rubber / TPE liners** | Rubber-specific cleaner + protector | Anything leaving a slick finish |

## The rule that saves your dashboard

**Never wipe dry dust off a hard surface.** Blow it off, brush it, or vacuum it first.

This matters most on **piano-black trim**, which is the most scratch-prone surface in a
modern car. Dragging dry dust across gloss black plastic fills it with fine scratches
that are effectively uncorrectable without polishing.

It is the same principle as the exterior: dry contact plus grit equals scratches. We
cover the full sequence in our
[interior detailing guide](/blog/perfect-interior-detail/).

## What we deliberately left out

**Air fresheners.** They mask smells while the cause continues. Persistent interior
odour is almost always damp carpet, something under a seat, or a neglected cabin air
filter — and a cabin filter is cheap enough that replacing it should be the first move,
not the last.

**All-in-one "interior detailer" sprays that promise shine.** Shine on a dashboard is
the problem, not the goal.

**Steam cleaners.** They work, but they are easy to misuse on trim adhesives and
electronics, and this is not the category to learn on.
