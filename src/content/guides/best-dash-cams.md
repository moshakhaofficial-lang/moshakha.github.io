Dash cam listings compete on resolution because it's a big number that's easy to print.
It is not the spec that determines whether the footage is any use.

## Resolution is oversold

The moment that matters is almost always in poor conditions — dusk, rain, an unlit road,
oncoming headlights. What determines whether a number plate is readable there is the
**sensor and lens aperture**, not the pixel count.

A 4K camera with a weak sensor gives you more pixels of the same unreadable smear. A 2K
camera with a modern low-light sensor gives you a legible plate.

4K also fills memory cards faster, generates more heat on a hot windscreen, and produces
files awkward enough that people stop pulling footage off.

**2K with a good sensor is the sweet spot.** Above that you're paying for a number.

## Supercapacitor, not battery

This is a hard requirement, and it's why every pick here has one.

A dash cam sits on a windscreen. Interior temperatures in summer sun get extreme.
Lithium batteries degrade rapidly under sustained heat, lose the ability to close a file
safely on shutdown, and carry a small but real swelling risk.

A supercapacitor holds just enough charge to finish writing the current file when power
cuts, tolerates heat far better, and lasts years longer.

**If a listing does not say supercapacitor, assume it has a battery.**

## Front and rear beats front and sharper

If you're choosing between a sharper single camera and two decent ones, take two.

A large share of contested incidents are rear-end collisions, and a front-only camera
records nothing useful in those. The second channel closes a coverage gap that no amount
of front resolution can.

## Parking mode needs hardwiring

Parking mode records while the car is off, which means drawing power while the car is
off.

Doing this from the 12V socket — where it works at all — risks a flat battery. Done
properly it's hardwired through a fuse box with a **voltage cut-off** that stops
recording when battery voltage drops below a set threshold.

That's a hardwire kit and either an afternoon or a fitter. If you're not doing that,
treat parking mode as unavailable rather than as a feature you have.

## How we chose

Specification analysis — sensor generation, aperture, capacitor versus battery, parking
mode implementation and channel count — plus aggregated long-term owner feedback with
attention to heat-related failure and firmware stability. We have not run comparative
night footage tests. No brand paid for placement.

## The two things that make footage useless

**A worn memory card.** Dash cams write continuously and loop-overwrite, which kills
standard cards fast. Use a high-endurance surveillance-rated card, and reformat it in
the camera every month or two. Card failure is the most common reason footage is missing
when someone finally needs it.

**A dirty windscreen.** The camera shoots through your glass, and the interior
plasticiser film scatters light straight into the lens at night. Clean the inside of the
screen properly — our [streak-free glass guide](/blog/glass-cleaning-mastery/) covers it,
and it's also what makes the adhesive mount actually stick.
