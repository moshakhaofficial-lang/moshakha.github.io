Walk through any detailing product listing and GSM is everywhere: 300, 500, 800, 1200,
1600. The number is always presented as a score, with higher implied to be better.

It isn't a score. **GSM measures density, not quality** — and once you understand what
it does and doesn't tell you, a lot of detailing marketing stops working on you.

## What GSM actually measures

GSM is grams per square metre: take one square metre of the fabric, weigh it, and that's
the number. That's the whole definition.

More grams means more fibre packed into the same area, which generally means a deeper
pile and more water capacity. Useful — but notice what GSM does **not** describe:

- **Fibre quality.** Cheap and premium fibres weigh the same.
- **Blend ratio.** The polyester/polyamide split drives absorbency, and GSM ignores it.
- **Weave type.** Twisted loop, plush and waffle can all hit the same GSM and behave
  completely differently.
- **Edge construction.** A hard stitched border on a 1200 GSM towel can mar paint that
  an edgeless 500 GSM towel never would.

Two towels at identical GSM can differ enormously. This is why GSM alone is a poor
buying signal — and why it's the number retailers advertise most.

## The specs that matter more than GSM

### Blend ratio (70/30 vs 80/20)

Microfibre is polyester and polyamide. Polyester does the scrubbing; **polyamide does
the absorbing**, and costs more.

- **80/20** — 20% polyamide. The budget standard. Perfectly serviceable.
- **70/30** — 30% polyamide. Noticeably more absorbent and softer at the same GSM.

A 70/30 towel at 900 GSM will generally out-absorb an 80/20 towel at 1100 GSM. Blend
ratio is the single most useful spec on a listing, and it is advertised far less often
than GSM — which should tell you something.

### Weave

- **Twisted loop** — long twisted fibres that pull water up into the pile. The correct
  weave for drying a vehicle.
- **Plush / high pile** — soft and deep, holds product well. Best for wax and sealant
  removal.
- **Waffle weave** — thin, low-lint, gridded. Best for glass.
- **Low-pile / flat** — dense and short. Best for interiors and final wipe-downs.

### Edge construction

Edgeless or silk-banded edges are safest on paint. A thick polyester overlock stitch is
harder than your clear coat and is a genuine marring risk on soft paint.

## GSM matched to the job

This is the table the "higher is better" crowd never publishes.

| GSM range | Pile | Right job | Wrong job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **200–350** | Very low | Glass, mirrors, final buff, engine bays | Drying panels — no capacity |
| **350–500** | Low | Interiors, door shuts, general purpose, applying product | Drying a whole car |
| **500–800** | Medium | All-rounder: wax removal, quick detailer, light drying | Streaks on glass |
| **800–1200** | High, usually twisted loop | **Drying panels** — the sweet spot for most vehicles | Anything needing precision |
| **1200–1600+** | Very high | Drying large SUVs, trucks, vans; two cars back to back | Glass, interiors, small cars |

## When high GSM is actively the wrong choice

Three cases where reaching for the biggest number makes your results worse:

**Glass.** A 1200 GSM towel on a windscreen is the number one cause of streaking. Deep
pile can't make consistent contact with a perfectly smooth surface, so it smears the
last film of moisture rather than lifting it. Drop to 300-400 GSM and the problem
disappears — no product change needed.

**Interiors.** Piano-black trim, screens and switchgear need a low-pile towel with
precision. A thick plush towel is clumsy, and its pile carries grit into panel gaps.

**Small cars.** On a hatchback, a 1600 GSM towel is heavy, slow, and awkward around
mirrors and badges. You are fighting the towel for no absorbency benefit you'll ever use.

## The honest summary

GSM is one spec among four that matter, and it is the least informative of them. In
rough order of importance for buying:

1. **Weave** — is it built for the job you're doing?
2. **Blend ratio** — 70/30 beats 80/20 at equal GSM.
3. **Edge construction** — edgeless or silk-banded on paint.
4. **GSM** — matched to the task, not maximised.

The reason you rarely read this is structural. Most detailing content is published by
companies that sell towels, and "our 1600 GSM towel is overkill for your Civic" is not a
sentence a shop can afford to write.
