Pressure washers are sold on PSI. It is in the model name, the headline and the first
line of every spec list.

There are two different PSI figures in circulation, they differ by about a quarter, and
manufacturers are inconsistent about which one they print. In one case we found two
machines from the same brand whose headline numbers sit 1.5% apart and whose rated
pressures sit 35% apart — with the weaker machine costing $61 more.

## Where this data comes from

**Every figure on this page was read from the manufacturer's own product listings on
29 October 2026.** We did not test any machine and we measured nothing. This is a record
of what two brands publish and how they label it.

- **Brands:** Sun Joe, via [shopjoe.com](https://shopjoe.com) (27 listings in the pressure
  washer collection), and Greenworks, via
  [greenworkstools.com](https://www.greenworkstools.com) (35 units after excluding
  accessories and open-box duplicates).
- **Two brands is a small sample,** and we are not claiming an industry-wide pattern. The
  central finding does not rest on sample size: it rests on Sun Joe stating both figures
  for the same machine in a single sentence, which is either true or it is not.
- **Accessories were excluded.** A Sun Joe extension lance lists "1800 to 4000 PSI" — that
  is a compatibility range for machines it fits, not a specification of anything. It was
  caught by product category and dropped.
- **Figures were taken from product titles and description text**, not from the rendered
  page, so a number appearing in a banner or a related-products block was never counted.
- **Prices** are as listed on those sites on 29 October 2026 and will have changed.
- The Sun Joe SPX4800's URL slug states "3200 PSI max, 1.3 GPM max" while its description
  states "up to 2500 rated PSI and up to 1.1 GPM." We treat the description as
  authoritative and note the discrepancy rather than resolving it.

## What the two PSI figures actually are

**Rated pressure** is what the pump sustains while water is flowing and you are cleaning
something. It is the number that describes the machine doing its job.

**Max pressure** is a peak measured at a different operating point — typically as flow
approaches zero. When you release the trigger, flow stops and pressure spikes against the
unloader valve. That spike is real, and it is not pressure you can clean with, because
cleaning requires flow.

So the two numbers describe different moments. Rated describes spraying. Max describes
very nearly not spraying.

Across the three Sun Joe units that publish both, rated pressure was **72% to 78% of
max**, median 75%. Put the other way: **the max figure runs about a third above the rated
figure.**

| Unit | Max PSI | Rated PSI | Rated as % of max |
|---|---|---|---|
| GO 2000 PSI Max | 2,000 | 1,500 | 75% |
| SPX205E-XT | 1,600 | 1,150 | 72% |
| SPX4800 | 3,200 | 2,500 | 78% |

These are not inferred. Sun Joe states both in one sentence — for the GO unit:
"2000 PSI max / 1500 PSI rated and 1.7 GPM max." For the SPX205E-XT: "1150 PSI rated
pressure (1600 PSI max) and 1.1 GPM rated flow."

## The comparison that matters

Sun Joe uses both naming conventions in the same catalog. Two machines, both listed on
29 October 2026:

| | Headline PSI | Rated PSI | Rated GPM | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPX3000 "Certified Rated 2030 PSI" | 2,030 | **2,030** | 1.2 | **$169.00** |
| GO "2000 PSI Max" | 2,000 | **1,500** | not published | **$229.99** |

On the shelf these look equivalent — 2030 against 2000, a 1.5% difference that reads as a
tie. On the figure that describes cleaning they are **35% apart**, and the weaker one
costs $61 more.

The SPX3000 is the machine in our
[pressure washer guide](/guides/best-pressure-washers-for-cars/) and we
[review it separately](/reviews/sun-joe-spx3000-pressure-washer/). Nothing here is a
criticism of the GO unit's build — it is a point about what the numbers on the two boxes
let you conclude, which is almost nothing.

## Why cleaning units break

The standard advice, and it is good advice, is to judge a pressure washer on **cleaning
units**: PSI × GPM. Pressure dislodges dirt, flow carries it away, and the product
captures both better than either figure alone.

The problem is what you multiply.

- **SPX3000:** 2,030 rated PSI × 1.2 rated GPM = **2,436 CU.** Both inputs share one
  basis, so this is a meaningful number.
- **GO 2000:** the published headline figures are 2,000 PSI max and 1.7 GPM max.
  Multiplying them gives **3,400 CU** — a number the machine cannot produce, because max
  pressure happens near zero flow and max flow happens at low pressure. They never occur
  together.

A buyer doing the arithmetic from the headline figures would conclude the GO is **40%
stronger**. On rated pressure the SPX3000 is **35% stronger**, and the GO's rated flow is
not published at all, so a legitimate cleaning-unit figure cannot be calculated for it.

Cleaning units work. They only work when both inputs are on the same basis, and the
listing has to tell you what that basis is.

## The other failure: no basis at all

Sun Joe at least labels its figures. The alternative is worse.

Of **35 Greenworks pressure washers**, 32 state both a PSI and a GPM figure right in the
product name — "2300 PSI 1.2 GPM Cold Water Electric Pressure Washer" is the house style,
and as a format that is genuinely more useful than PSI alone.

But **29 of the 35 state no measurement basis anywhere** — not in the title, not in the
description. Neither "max" nor "rated". So you have two precise-looking numbers and no way
to know what either refers to, which means no way to compare them against a competitor
that does say.

Neither of us can tell you whether Greenworks' "2300 PSI" is a rated or a max figure. That
is the finding.

## What to do when buying

**Look for the word.** "Rated" or "certified rated" is the figure you want. "Max" is
roughly a third higher than it.

**If you only see a max figure,** mentally take about a quarter off to estimate rated
pressure. Our three data points put rated at 72–78% of max, which is thin but it is what
is published.

**If you see no basis at all,** you cannot compare that machine with one that states its
basis. Check the manual, which often gives the rated figure even when marketing does not.

**For washing cars, stop worrying about PSI above roughly 1500 rated.** Flow carries grit
away and that is what protects your paint; pressure beyond that point mostly adds risk of
forcing water past trim and seals. Nozzle choice matters more than the last 500 PSI —
[pressure washer nozzle angles](/blog/pressure-washer-nozzle-angles-for-cars/) covers
which tip to use, and [the foam cannon guide](/how-to/foam-cannon-guide/) covers the part
of the job that actually does the cleaning.

## What we did not establish

**We did not test these machines,** so we cannot tell you what either one produces at the
nozzle. Everything here is what the manufacturers publish about themselves.

**We did not establish an industry pattern.** Two brands is a small sample. Several other
makers do not publish structured product data we could read, so this is a documented
practice at two brands rather than a survey of the category.

**We cannot say whether Greenworks' unlabelled figures are max or rated.** If they are
rated, their machines are stronger than a like-for-like comparison with Sun Joe's max
figures would suggest. If they are max, the reverse. The absence of the label is the whole
problem and we are not going to guess past it.

This is the second of these surveys. The first found that across 46 jump starters from four
brands, [not one published cranking amps](/guides/jump-starter-amp-ratings/) — the one
standardized figure in that category.
