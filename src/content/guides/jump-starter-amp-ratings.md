Every jump starter is sold on an amp figure. It is on the box, in the model name, and
first in every spec list. We wanted to know what that number is actually based on, so we
went through what four manufacturers publish for 46 products.

The short version: the number every jump starter is sold on is the one number nobody
defines, and the number that would let you compare them is almost never published.

## Where this data comes from

**All figures on this page were read from each manufacturer's own public product pages on
29 October 2026.** We did not test any product, we did not measure anything, and we make
no claim about how any of these units perform. This is a survey of published
specifications — what each brand chooses to state and what it leaves out.

Specifically:

- **Brands and counts:** Schumacher (21 products), GOOLOO (13), Hulkman (7), NOCO (5).
  46 in total. These four were chosen because each publishes a full product range on its
  own site.
- **Sources:** [no.co](https://no.co), [us.gooloo.com](https://us.gooloo.com),
  [hulkman.com](https://www.hulkman.com) and
  [schumacherelectric.com](https://schumacherelectric.com). Figures came from each site's
  own product data, not from a retailer listing, and not from Amazon.
- **Amp figures** were taken from the manufacturer's own model designation, which is where
  all four brands put them.
- **A specification is recorded as absent** only when it was missing from that product's
  own description text. We do not count a figure as published if it appeared elsewhere on
  the page, such as in a banner or a related-products block.
- **Prices** are as published on those sites on 29 October 2026 and will have changed.
  Schumacher does not sell direct, so no prices are included for its products.
- **Our own correction:** an earlier draft of this analysis reported that one brand claimed
  roughly twice the amps per liter of rated engine capacity as the others. That was wrong.
  It compared one brand's diesel ratings against other brands' gas ratings. Corrected to a
  like-for-like basis, the difference disappears. We describe this in full under
  [what we did not find](#what-we-did-not-find), because the mistake is an easy one and
  worth other people avoiding.

## What the four brands publish

| Published figure | Products stating it | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Headline amp figure | 46 of 46 | 100% |
| Rated engine size (liters) | 30 of 46 | 65% |
| Battery capacity (Wh or mAh) | 3 of 46 | 7% |
| **Cranking amps (the standardized measure)** | **0 of 46** | **0%** |

## Finding 1: nobody publishes the standardized number

Cranking amps is a defined measurement. It specifies the current a battery can sustain for
a set period at a set temperature, which is what makes figures from different products
comparable with each other.

Peak amps has no such definition. There is no enforced standard for the duration over
which it is measured, the temperature, or the load. Two manufacturers can test similar
hardware and publish very different numbers without either being wrong, because there is
no shared definition of what is being claimed.

Across all 46 products, from all four brands, **not one published a cranking amp figure.**
The measurement that would allow comparison is the measurement nobody states.

## Finding 2: four brands, four different labels

The brands do not even agree on what to call the headline figure.

| Brand | How the amp figure is labeled |
|---|---|
| NOCO | "RATED AT 1000A" — no qualifier at all |
| Schumacher | "peak amps" (14 of 21 products) |
| Hulkman | "peak current" (5 of 7 products) |
| GOOLOO | "Peak Current" on some products, a bare amp figure on others |

NOCO's wording is the most interesting. "Rated at 1000A" sounds like a specification
against a standard, and no standard is named. Hulkman's "peak current" and Schumacher's
"peak amps" at least signal that the figure is an instantaneous maximum rather than a
sustained one.

When four brands use four labels for the same slot on the spec sheet, the figures in that
slot are not a common unit.

## Finding 3: capacity is almost never published

Stored energy — watt-hours, or milliamp-hours — is the figure that would make these packs
genuinely comparable. A pack holding more energy has more cranking attempts in it, and
watt-hours is a real physical unit that means the same thing everywhere.

It appears on **3 of 46 products**:

- Hulkman Alpha Storm — 64Wh / 20,000mAh
- GOOLOO GT6000 — 27,000mAh
- GOOLOO GE4500 — 16,000mAh

NOCO publishes it for none of its five consumer units. Schumacher publishes it for none of
its 21.

## Finding 4: price does not reliably track the amp figure

Within GOOLOO's own range, on the prices published on 29 October 2026:

| Model | Claimed amps | Price |
|---|---|---|
| GE4500 | 4,500A | $59.99 |
| A3 Lite | 2,500A | $59.99 |
| GP2000 | 2,000A | $69.99 |
| GP4000 | 4,000A | $89.99 |
| GP3000 | 3,000A | $99.99 |
| GT6000 | 6,000A | $129.99 |
| GT TRUCK PRO | 10,000A | $239.99 |

A 4,500A unit sells for $40 less than a 3,000A unit from the same brand. If the amp figure
tracked a consistent physical property, that ordering would be difficult to explain.

For contrast, NOCO's and Hulkman's ranges are internally consistent — amps, rated engine
size, weight and price all rise together:

| NOCO | Amps | Gas | Diesel | Weight | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GB20 | 500A | 4.0L | — | 2.4 lb | $79.95 |
| GB40 | 1,000A | 6.0L | 3.0L | 2.4 lb | $99.95 |
| GB50 | 1,500A | 7.0L | 4.5L | 2.7 lb | $149.95 |
| GB70 | 2,000A | 8.0L | 6.0L | 5.0 lb | $199.95 |
| GB150 | 3,000A | 9.0L | 7.0L | 7.5 lb | $299.95 |

That is what a coherent ladder looks like. It is worth saying plainly that both NOCO and
Hulkman present one.

## The gas and diesel trap

Of the 26 products publishing both a gas and a diesel rating, **the diesel figure was
between 46% and 80% of the gas figure, with a median of 67%.**

A diesel has no spark plug and relies on compressing air hard enough to ignite fuel, so
the starter turns the engine against far greater resistance. Every manufacturer therefore
rates the same unit for a smaller diesel than gas engine.

The practical consequence: if you compare one product's diesel rating against another's gas
rating, you will overstate the second product by roughly a third. This is exactly the error
we made in our own first pass.

## What we did not find

We expected to find that the cheapest brand inflated its amp figures relative to the
established ones. **We could not demonstrate that, and our first attempt to do so was
wrong.**

Dividing claimed amps by rated engine capacity gives a rough index of how aggressively a
brand rates its hardware. On a like-for-like diesel basis:

| Brand | Products | Amps per liter (diesel rating) |
|---|---|---|
| Schumacher | 16 | 167–375 |
| Hulkman | 6 | 292–400 |
| NOCO | 4 | 333–429 |
| GOOLOO | 2 | 400–500 |

The ranges overlap. NOCO's GB150 sits at 429 A/L, between GOOLOO's two verifiable units at
400 and 500. GOOLOO publishes a liter rating for only 2 of its 13 products, which is too
few to support any claim about the brand.

So: **no defensible difference between these brands in how aggressively they rate.** The
problem with the amp figure is not that one brand abuses it. The problem is that it is
undefined for everyone.

## What to buy on instead

**Use the rated engine size.** 30 of the 46 products publish it, and it is a claim about
the job rather than about an unstandardized measurement. A manufacturer saying a unit
starts gas engines up to 8.0L is telling you something you can act on.

**Match it to your engine, with headroom.** Cold weather thickens oil and reduces what a
battery delivers, which is when an undersized unit fails. Our
[jump starter guide](/guides/best-jump-starters/) sizes by engine for that reason.

**Use weight as a sanity check.** Mass is a crude proxy for cell count. NOCO's 3,000A unit
weighs 7.5 lb. A pack claiming more amps at a fraction of the weight is not obviously
delivering more current.

**Check the protection circuitry, not the amps.** Reverse-polarity and spark protection are
what matter in the one situation you will use the device — in the dark, flustered, with the
manual long gone. That is the real argument for
[the NOCO GB40](/reviews/noco-boost-gb40-jump-starter/) over a higher-amp alternative, and
we say the same thing in our
[GOOLOO GP4000 review](/reviews/gooloo-gp4000-jump-starter/).
