Car shampoo is the cheapest part of a wash and one of the easiest to get wrong — mostly
by reaching for whatever's under the kitchen sink.

## Why dish soap ruins paint protection

Dish soap is engineered to strip oils and grease. That's exactly what it does to your
wax or sealant, in a single wash, along with drying out rubber seals and plastic trim
over time.

It also has no lubricity. Car shampoo is slick on purpose — that slickness is what
allows a trapped particle to slide across the panel instead of cutting into it. Dish soap
cleans and offers nothing on that front, which makes swirl marks more likely even with
good technique.

There is one legitimate use: deliberately stripping old wax before polishing or applying
a new sealant. If that's not what you're doing, use car shampoo.

## What pH-neutral means

A pH-neutral shampoo lifts dirt without chemically attacking whatever protection is on
the paint. Strongly alkaline cleaners strip wax and sealant — genuinely useful when you
want a clean slate before correction, and destructive when you've just spent an afternoon
waxing.

Every pick above is pH-neutral and safe over wax, sealant and ceramic coatings.

## The honest ranking of what matters

Shampoo is genuinely the least important variable in whether your wash damages paint.
In order of actual impact:

1. **Not using a brush-type automatic wash**
2. **[Two buckets with grit guards](/how-to/two-bucket-car-wash-method)**
3. **[A mitt rather than a sponge](/how-to/wash-mitt-vs-sponge)**
4. **Washing top to bottom in straight lines**
5. **Drying properly and immediately**
6. Shampoo choice

Any pH-neutral shampoo used with good technique beats the most expensive bottle on the
shelf used with one bucket and a sponge. Buy a decent one, then spend your attention on
the steps above it.
